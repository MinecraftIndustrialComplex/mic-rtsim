# Worm mods for the underground threat near oil (research, 2026-10-20)

Ben's ask (OG-9, DECISIONS.md): replace the sculk and warden gate with Terraria-style underground
worms that demolish players and their industry near oil fields, spare the oil pipes, and wreck
subterranean Create machinery, pumps and other works.

Target: Minecraft 1.21.1, NeoForge 21.1.x, Create 6 pack. Sinytra Connector is in the pack.

How this was done: Modrinth API searches (about 60 queries, with and without the 1.21.1 and neoforge
facets), CurseForge via web search, then jars downloaded into `/tmp/worm-research/<mod>/`, unzipped and
read with `javap` and `strings`. Nothing from those jars was run. Jars of the pack's own mods were
scanned too (below).

## 1. Short answer

Nothing fits perfectly. No 1.21.1 NeoForge mod gives an ambient, stone-tunnelling, base-wrecking worm
that also spares well pipes by itself. The closest is **Terra Entity** (the Terraria monster module of
Confluence): its worms are real multipart worms that pass through terrain and break no blocks at all,
which is exactly the "bounded sim damage" shape rtsim already uses. Its cost is content and mixin
weight, not the worm. The lightweight alternative is **Aftershock** (Tremors graboids), which fits the
desert oil biome but is a single-body creature that burrows only in loose soil unless a tag is widened.

Ranked (details in section 3):

1. Terra Entity: best worm, heaviest mod.
2. Aftershock: light, tag-driven, wrong shape (not segmented), loose soil only.
3. Desert Behemoths: Sandworms (1.21.1 unofficial port): spectacular, but a desert-surface Dune worm.

Nothing in the pack already fits (section 5).

## 4. Recommendation

### 4.1 Pick

**Terra Entity, using only its `BaseWorm` family** (start with `devourer` and `giant_worm`, step up to
`bone_serpent` for the deep tier). It is the only 1.21.1 NeoForge worm found that is truly multipart,
goes through terrain, and breaks nothing on its own. That last property is the point: Ben wants the
well pipes spared and player works hit, and rtsim already decides damage itself (I23, P28: bounded sim
damage). A worm that never breaks blocks makes the plugin the single owner of what breaks, so sparing
pipes is a filter in the plugin, not a patch to someone else's mod.

Conditions on the pick, which Ben should weigh (D1 and the dislike of content):
- It drags in a large content mod. The mitigation is not to hide it but to keep it inert: stop its
  natural spawns, leave its NPCs and weapons uncraftable or unspawned. How much of that is possible
  without patching is UNVERIFIED (section 2.2). If it is not enough, the fallback is option 2 of 4.4.
- Its mixins (24 common) touch spawning, chunk tracking and block state. This needs a boot test with
  Sable, Create Aeronautics, Distant Horizons and the other mixin-heavy mods in the pack.

### 4.2 How the rtsim plugin would use it

A new plugin, `mic_rtsim_terraentity` (D4: plugins may be built around one mod; the core stays free of
it). The plugin owns four things:

1. **Trigger (player activity underground near an oil field).** The sim already produces the signals
   (OILGATE §4.5): the I2 census of districts, `BlockEvent.EntityPlaceEvent` naming the placing player,
   and `IND_DEPOSIT_SAMPLE` for chunks with a pumpjack hole. Add one more `Encroachment` kind,
   "works underground at a field": a player-placed block or machine, or a player present, below the
   local surface by a TUNABLE depth, inside an oil chunk or within a TUNABLE radius of one. Severity
   accumulates per district per day like the dragon rows, so one torch does not summon a worm but a
   mine, a pump room or a base does. Digging down the well shaft to oil from below counts, which is the
   cheese P13 item 3 exists to stop.
2. **Spawn (puppet).** Worms are sim agents with a class (new: Burrower) and the engine owns the truth
   (P3). When a player is near, the plugin materializes the agent as a puppet: create the worm type,
   place the head in solid rock a TUNABLE distance (for example 24 to 48 blocks) from the player's
   works and outside the exclusion volume of any well shaft, mark it persistent and tag it as a puppet
   so vanilla never saves it (parts are not saved anyway), and let it start in `idle`. Per-field
   concurrency is capped at 1 or 2. Strength tier (devourer, giant_worm, bone_serpent, wither) follows
   the district's accumulated severity.
3. **Steer.** Two layers, both vanilla `Mob` API, to be confirmed by GameTest:
   - Coarse: `setTarget(player)` and rely on its own `ComeAndBackDashAttackGoal` and
     `NearestAttackableTargetGoal`, bounded by the sim to a leash around the field (the plugin teleports
     or discards a worm that leaves it).
   - Fine: the plugin adds its own highest-priority `Goal` through the public `goalSelector` that
     drives the head toward a plugin-chosen waypoint (the nearest surveyed works, then the next), since
     the head's movement is ordinary `Mob` motion plus `noPhysics`. Waypoints come from the district's
     surveyed block set (INDUSTRY §13.7).
4. **Bounded damage, sparing the well pipes.** The worm breaks no blocks (verified), so every broken
   block is the plugin's decision. Each pass, the plugin takes the blocks within a TUNABLE radius of the
   head, intersects them with the district's surveyed player-placed set, removes the protected set, caps
   the count per pass and per visit (I23's cap and pacing, I25), and breaks the rest with
   `level.destroyBlock(pos, true)` so contents drop as items. The protected set:
   - the well shaft: every block in the column below each `createdieselgenerators:pumpjack_hole` down to
     bedrock, and the hole itself (OILGATE §3.1: the hole scans down "through pipes"; the exact pipe
     block ids are not yet listed in the docs, so confirm them in the CDG jar), and
   - a datapack block tag `#mic_rtsim:worm_immune` that lists those pipe blocks, bedrock, and
     `createdieselgenerators:oil_deposit`, so tuning is data, not code.
   Pumps, Create machines, drills, shafts, tanks and belts are in the surveyed set and are fair game, as
   Ben asked. A destroyed machine stops an abstract run (I23). Unwatched, the same selection applies
   abstractly and is applied on load under the block quota (C18); the watched and unwatched outcomes
   agree (P28). Fire and explosions are never used.
5. **Retreat.** A worm leaves when any of these holds: its per-visit block quota is spent, no surveyed
   works remain in reach, the player has left the field's radius for a dwell, a hard lifetime
   (TUNABLE) expires, or health is low. The plugin clears the target, steers the head down and away
   along a vector, and discards the puppet once it is a TUNABLE distance below the player's works or
   beyond tracking range. The sim agent returns to dormant with a cooldown, so there is no worm in
   permanent attendance. Retreat depth uses the same `noPhysics` motion, so it visibly dives.

All numbers above marked TUNABLE are placeholders to be set from GameTest and the sim's bench, not
findings.

### 4.3 Tests the plugin would need first (to turn UNVERIFIED into verified)

1. A GameTest that spawns `giant_worm` below ground, sets a player target 30 blocks away in stone, and
   records head depth and path (does it tunnel to the target or skim the surface?).
2. A boot test of Terra Entity alone, then in the full pack, with timings of server tick and load.
3. A test that the natural spawns can be silenced with the biome modifier override and config.
4. A test of contact damage against a villager, an rtsim puppet and a Create contraption rider.

### 4.4 If Terra Entity fails those tests

Closest options, in order of preference, not recommended as final:
1. **Aftershock graboid plus datapack.** Widen `#aftershock:graboid_burrowable` to stone and deepslate
   (it is a tag), extend `#aftershock:heat_blocks` with Create machines (it is a tag), and drop the
   spawn tags so the plugin alone places them. Gains: small footprint, desert-native (matches the oil
   biome rule), breaks heat blocks itself, config health and damage. Loses: not segmented, not
   Terraria-shaped, and its own block breaking is only partly tag-bounded (path breaking is not), so
   the plugin would still need to filter. Licence conflict (MIT vs CC-BY-NC-4.0) to settle.
2. **A reskinned existing mob.** Pack has Entity Model Features and Entity Texture Features, so a
   resource pack can reskin an existing multipart mob. The Ice and Fire death worm is already in the
   pack, multipart, and configurable in health and damage, but it can swim only through blocks in
   `BlockTags.SAND` (hard-coded vanilla tag), so it cannot enter stone without changing vanilla's
   sand tag, which has other users. Not recommended.
3. **A custom worm entity.** CustomHitboxLib (Modrinth `customhitboxlib`, MIT, 953 downloads, NeoForge
   1.21.1) offers multipart hitboxes on any entity, which would cut the work, but a bespoke entity
   conflicts with D3 ("only if absolutely necessary"). This is Ben's call, and only if sections 4.3
   tests fail and Aftershock is rejected.

### 4.5 Why not the others

- Desert Behemoths port: a Dune sandworm for open desert, made for spectacle (shaders, screen shake,
  thumpers); needs Lodestone and AAA Particles which the pack lacks; no evidence it enters stone.
  It could still serve as a surface desert raid body later, separate from this question.
- Devourer of Gods, Bosses'Rise, Alex's Mobs: bosses or large content mods, not an ambient threat.
- Enter Sandworm: wrong biome, Fabric-only jar.
- Sculk and wardens: Ben rejected (OG-9).

| `NaturalSpawnerMixin`, HEAD on `isValidSpawnPostitionForType` | per spawn attempt | an empty method body | 0 samples |
| confluence_magic_lib `MobCategory` and `NaturalSpawner` mixins | spawn cycle; per-chunk data refreshed every 100 ticks | scales mob cap and spawn rate by the player attributes `MOB_SPAWN_COUNT` and `MOB_SPAWN_SPEED_MULTIPLIER`, both 1.0 unless a Terra item changes them, so identity | 0 samples (see the caveat below) |
| `AttributeInstanceMixin`, RETURN of `setDirty`, plus a `double` and a `boolean` field per `AttributeInstance` | on attribute changes | a field write | 0 samples; the fields cost about 16 B per attribute instance, inside the heap figure |
| Server-tick listeners: `CameraShakeManager.serverTick`, magic_lib `ServerTickEvent.Post` | once per tick | walk empty lists | 0 samples |
| Event listeners on damage, death, drops, join and leave, spawn finalising, equipment change, start tracking (Terra Entity 10, magic_lib 8) | per event | type checks that find no Terra Entity mob or item; the zombie slime-jockey roll on `EntityJoinLevelEvent` is the one with an effect (section 7.4) | 0 samples |

| Load-time only: `RegistryDataLoaderMixin`, `LootDataManagerMixin` (wraps `Map.put` in every JSON reload listener), `RecipeManagerMixin` (a regex check per recipe against an empty pattern list), `ReloadableServerResourcesMixin`, `ModifiableBiomeInfoMixin`, `MobSpawnSettingsBuilderMixin`, plus 1,169 classes, 231 items, 120-odd entity types and their attribute tables | boot and `/reload` | | inside the boot and metaspace figures |
| Rare: `PlayerMixin.canInteractWithBlock` HEAD, `ZombieMixin` NBT read and write TAIL, boss-bar mixins, `AbstractContainerMenuMixin` | player interaction, zombie save and load, boss bars, menus | type checks | 0 samples |

All Terra Entity frames together came to **0.27%, 0.40% and 0.39% of the server thread** in the three
ground-scene profiles (rounds 1, 2 and 6) and **0.20%** in the ship profile, against a round-to-round spread
of about 2% in the tick time. Nothing else in the B profiles belongs to Terra Entity or its libraries.

Caveat: no player was connected to the test servers (they run unattended). That leaves out per-player paths:
`ServerEntity` pairing, magic_lib's `PlayerEvent.StartTracking` and `PlayerNaturalHealEvent` listeners and its
per-player spawn data, and the `canInteractWithBlock` mixin. Each is a type check or a lookup per event, the
same kind as the measured rows, so with a handful of players they are expected to stay under the 0.01% line,
but that is read from the bytecode, not measured.

The shimmer hook is the one that shows in a microbenchmark: a fixed 2 to 3 ns on every collision query
whatever the context, 14 to 25% of a call that vanilla answers in 10 to 21 ns (table in 7.1; the benchmark
times 25 rounds of 2 million calls over the 4,913 real block states of a 17x17x17 cube at spawn, in the
running server, A and B at the same moment). That sounds large but the call is a small part of a tick: the
ground scene makes 47,217 calls per tick, so the hook adds 0.09 to 0.14 ms to a tick of 260 to 500 ms (0.02
to 0.05%), and idle it adds about 10 µs to a 3 ms tick. `getDestroySpeed`, which Terra Entity does not hook,
is the control and came out equal.

### 7.3 Client cost (estimate, not measured)

No client was run: there is no display on the test machine and no way to drive a NeoForge 1.21.1 client
here. The numbers below come from counting the jar's assets and classes and from the server measurements, and
are an estimate.

| Item | Estimate for a client with Terra Entity silenced | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Startup and resource reload | **+2 to 5 s** on a mid-range PC, perhaps double on a potato | 1,650 classes (Terra Entity 1,169, magic_lib 209, ParticleStorm 214, ThreeDimensionParticle 53); GeckoLib parses all 177 geo models (2.4 MB of JSON, 35 of them encrypted and decrypted first by Terra Entity's file loader mixins) and 148 animation files (1.4 MB) at every resource reload, whether or not the mob ever appears; 207 item textures and the mod's shaders are added; the server measured +3.6 s of boot (-0.5 to +5.6) for the common half |
| Heap | **+30 to 50 MB** | the server's +20 MB live heap, plus GeckoLib's baked models and animations (10 to 20 MB) and renderer and particle registries |
| Metaspace | **+12 to 16 MB** | the server's +8.6 MB plus about 170 client classes and 41 client mixins (12 in Terra Entity, 29 in the libraries with Sodium present, as in this pack) |
| VRAM | **about +9 MB** | an unused full-screen `TextureTarget` that `GameRendererMixin` allocates and resizes with the window (1920x1080x4 bytes = 8.3 MB; 4 times that at 4K); 207 item sprites in the item atlas (89k pixels, about 0.5 MB with mipmaps). The 271 entity textures (9.1 Mpx, 35 MB uncompressed) load only when an entity is drawn, so 0 with worms silenced |
| Frame time | **a few microseconds per frame** | the per-frame listeners (`RenderLevelStageEvent` at three stages, `RenderGuiLayerEvent.Pre`, `RenderHandEvent`, `RenderLivingEvent` for players, two client-tick listeners) each check an empty list or a held item and return. The exception is `CameraShakeManager.handleCameraShake`, below. The 41 client mixins (GeckoLib bone hooks in ParticleStorm, item and entity rendering, shader setup) are null checks on the paths they touch |
| JEI | 231 items and 120-odd spawn eggs added to the ingredient list | hidden with the section 7.4 JEI fix |

**One thing to check in a client before shipping, and it is not a performance item.**
`CameraShakeManager.handleCameraShake` listens to `ViewportEvent.ComputeCameraAngles` (every frame, no
condition) and, before it looks at its shake list, does this every frame, inside a
`catch (Exception)`:

```java
Method m = Camera.class.getDeclaredMethod("setPosition", double.class, double.class, double.class);
m.setAccessible(true);
Vec3 p = Minecraft.getInstance().player.position();
m.invoke(event.getCamera(), p.x, p.y + 5.0, p.z);
```

NeoForge 1.21.1 runs on Mojang names, so `Camera.setPosition(double, double, double)` exists, and a probe in
the test server showed that `setAccessible` plus `invoke` from a mod into Minecraft's module works (2.2 to
5.3 microseconds a call, the frame cost in the table). If nothing later overrides it, the camera would sit
5 blocks above the player's feet in every view, all the time. It reads like debug code left in. This was not
seen in a client and no public report of it was found, so the first player who joins with the mod will
settle it in a second. If it is real, the fix is a two-line addition to a pack-side mod:
`NeoForge.EVENT_BUS.unregister(CameraShakeManager.class)` on the client (the server tick listener in the same
class only drives worm screen shake, which is moot with worms silenced), or a mixin that cancels the method.

### 7.4 Gameplay leaks with the silencing datapack, and the fixes

Measured on a third server (B's mods and datapack, plus a probe mod that rolls a loot table through
`LootTable.getRandomItems`, the path `/loot` and chests use, global loot modifiers included). Each table was
rolled 2,000 times, mob tables with and without a (fake) player killer. Control: Legendary Survival Overhaul's own
modifier shows up where it should (723 heart fragments in 2,000 `bastion_treasure` rolls), so modifiers do run on
this path.

| Leak | As shipped, silenced | With the fixes below |
|---|---|---|
| Staff items in vanilla chests (village weaponsmith, ancient city, bastion treasure, woodland mansion, desert pyramid archaeology) | **None**: 0 Terra Entity items in 2,000 rolls of each | 0 |
| Piglin bartering, elder guardian drops | **None** (0 in 2,000 each, elder guardian with and without a player kill) | 0 |
| Other vanilla mob drops | **None** (zombie, 2,000 player kills: rotten flesh, carrot, potato, iron only) | 0 |
| Terra Entity enchantments on vanilla loot | **Yes.** `summoner_pact` (any helmet), `whip_sweep`, `multi_boomerang` (books): stronghold library 153 per 2,000 rolls, end city 68 enchanted helmets, ancient city 53, bastion 40 helmets, woodland mansion 22, dungeon 17. A level-30 helmet roll over `#in_enchanting_table` (what the enchanting table draws from) got `summoner_pact` 59 times in 400 (15%); a random-enchant book 21 in 400 (5%) | **0** everywhere |
| Slime jockeys on vanilla zombies | **Yes.** 26 of 500 freshly summoned adult zombies and husks (5.2%) got a Terra Entity `blue_slime` riding them (zombified piglins get `lava_slime`); config, not data, so the datapack cannot stop it | **0** |
| Curios slots on every player | **Yes**: `pet`, `light_pet`, `mount` | Not assigned to players (slot types stay registered, empty) |
| Recipes | 23 craftable (22 `terra_entity:*` plus `minecraft:flamarang_smithing`) | **0** (KubeJS: 1,528 removed against 1,505 without the script) |
| Items in JEI and creative | 231 items and 120-odd spawn eggs and entities | Hidden from JEI by `VOIDED_MOD_IDS` (client side, not run here) |
| Natural spawns, worldgen | None (section 5.1) | None |

Why the staff modifiers are dead: NeoForge only runs loot modifiers listed in
`data/neoforge/loot_modifiers/global_loot_modifiers.json`, and Terra Entity's shipped list names only its 20
`terra_curio` drop modifiers (which target `terra_entity:entities/*` tables, so they cannot touch vanilla mobs,
and 15 of their tables fail to parse without Terra Curio). The 7 staff modifiers exist as files but are not listed.
Nothing to do about them today. If a later Terra Entity lists them, the same datapack can override each of the 7
files (`data/terra_entity/loot_modifiers/{chest,gameplay,entities}/spawn_*_staff.json`) with
`{"neoforge:conditions": [{"type": "neoforge:false"}]}`, the form NeoForge's loader skips (read from the NeoForge
jar, not run here).

The fixes, all tested above except the JEI line:

1. `pack/config/terra_entity-server.toml` (Terra Entity registers a SERVER config, which NeoForge 21.1 reads
   from `config/`): `chance_to_spawn_slime_on_zombie_head = 0.0`. The other settings that could leak are already
   off (`enhance_all_monster = false`; attribute and armour configs target only Terra Entity mobs).
2. `pack/kubejs/server_scripts/terra_entity_inert.js`:

   ```js
   if (Platform.isLoaded('terra_entity')) {
     ServerEvents.recipes(event => {
       event.remove({ mod: 'terra_entity' })
       event.remove({ output: '@terra_entity' })
     })
     ServerEvents.tags('enchantment', event => {
       event.remove('minecraft:non_treasure', ['terra_entity:multi_boomerang', 'terra_entity:whip_sweep', 'terra_entity:summoner_pact'])
     })
   }
   ```

   The three enchantments reach vanilla loot, the enchanting table, trades and mob spawn equipment only through
   Terra Entity's addition to `#minecraft:non_treasure`; removing them there removes all four routes.
3. A datapack (the same one as the silencing overrides) with
   `data/terra_entity/curios/entities/common.json` = `{"entities": [], "slots": []}`.
4. `pack/kubejs/startup_scripts/voided_content.js`: add `'terra_entity'` to `global.VOIDED_MOD_IDS`, which the
   pack's `client_scripts/voided_jei.js` already uses to hide a whole mod from JEI.

### 7.5 Verdict

**Performance is not a reason to keep Terra Entity out.** Silenced, it costs about what one mid-sized content
mod costs, and nothing per tick that can be measured.

- **Potato server** (say 4 cores and a 4 to 6 GB heap): about **+3 to 4 s of boot**, **+9 MB metaspace** and
  **+20 MB of live heap**, roughly 1.5% on top of what the pack already holds (1.6 GB of heap and 400 MB of
  metaspace after boot). Tick time: no difference beyond the 2% gap that an A/A control shows between two
  identical servers, idle or with 2,000 sheep on the ground or on a Sable ship. The profiles put Terra
  Entity's share at 0.2 to 0.4% of the server thread, and it scales with the number of entities (the
  bundled library's entity-tick listener) and collision queries (the shimmer hook), not with anything
  Terra Entity adds; on a server that ticks in 20 ms that is well under 0.1 ms. That share never drops to
  zero while the jar is loaded, but it stays a fraction of a percent.
- **Potato client** (estimate, section 7.3): **+2 to 5 s** of startup and resource reload on a mid-range PC,
  maybe twice that on a potato; **+30 to 50 MB** of heap; **about 9 MB of VRAM** at 1080p, most of it a
  full-screen buffer that is never drawn; microseconds per frame. On a 4 GB client that is noticeable at
  startup and nowhere else.
- **Before it ships**, two things matter more than any of the above: the gameplay fixes in 7.4 (the mod is
  not inert as shipped: its enchantments reach vanilla loot and the enchanting table, 5% of zombies get a slime
  rider, players get three curios slots, and 23 recipes stay craftable), and the camera check in 7.3, which
  one minute in a client settles.
