# Glass box G2 choices (glass-box replay)

Every reversible choice made while building phase G2 (GLASSBOX.md §11.3), with ids G2-1,
G2-2, ... Anything that is Ben's call is in "For Ben", recommended option first. G1's choices
are in docs/choices-glassbox-g1.md (GB1 to GB111). Code comments cite these ids.

## The replay

| Id | Choice |
|---|---|
| G2-1 | **Report header 41.** The glass report (docs/industry-boundary.md §9) grows from 33 to 41 header slots: 33 replay model (0 none, 1 black box, 2 glass), 34 since (tick, -1 never), 35 regimes, 36 breakpoints, 37 oscillation fallbacks, 38 cut edges, 39 eligible (0/1), 40 learned sources. Good rows follow at 41. Java's `GlassReport.HEADER` matches. |
| G2-2 | **Eligibility (G-B4).** A compile replays on the glass model once one window of it has passed and no IND_EDIT came after (`Compiled::eligible`). Before that, and after an edit until the recompile passes, the district freezes (no replay; the books stay as the last reconcile left them). No compile, or one the compiler refused, keeps the black box. A derate or a demotion after a pass keeps eligibility; the replay solves with the compile's regime, so it uses the derated factors and the demoted kinds. |
| G2-3 | **Per-store pools.** A glass replay books into `PoolKey::Store(district, inventory, good)`, one pool per store and good (§4.1), read from each covered inventory's stock at the start. A store's change stays with that store and lands there at the reconcile; a change that cannot be placed in its own store goes to the district's owed (`reconcile::plan` spreads it as before). |
| G2-4 | **One Box process per part per regime.** Each part (G2-22) of a district's replay is one process: the regime's net rate per pool, as Box terms, all-or-nothing. A breakpoint stops it the tick before a flag changes; the replay re-reads the flags from the pool levels, re-solves (the regime cache answers most of these) and replaces the process in place. No slot period (C28). |
| G2-5 | **Held and room.** A store's good is flagged empty when its pool cannot run one tick of its drain; it clears when the pool has risen by the good's holding threshold above the flagged level, or (G2-19) holds stock while nothing arrives. Room is the store's free item slots, shared among the goods arriving in it (G2-26), and for fluids its capacity. A store some arriving good has no room in for a tick is flagged full and its in-edges close. |
| G2-6 | **Latency holds (§4.4).** A simulated node whose potential rises (its flow in the regime with nothing held) is held off for its downstream latency (the time its output takes to reach a store; a loop counts whole), then runs. What it makes arrives no earlier than in Create, and every flow stays a solve's, so goods are conserved. A fall is at once. |
| G2-7 | **Breakpoints and the oscillation limit.** A breakpoint is a pool at a bound (empty, full, a controller threshold, a dry cut good), a heater's phase timer, a hold coming due, or an outside event. More than `glass.breakpointsPerEventMax` (64) breakpoints since the last outside event or the last closed-form cycle is Oscillating. A hold coming due does not count (a node holds once per rise of its potential, and a rise needs another breakpoint), which is why the bench saw fallbacks at 99 to 110 breakpoints. A fallback (Oscillating, Unsettled, Stuck, Unexpected) moves the district to the black box until it is live again or a new compile arrives; with nothing learned it freezes. |
| G2-8 | **Residual node (G-B13, §5.1).** From the passing window, per unbounded good (a black box's): measured, shaded toward 0 by z sigma, minus predicted. Per bounded good, only a production above U (G2-57). Exact goods take none. It is split over the covered stores whose own window slope misses the same way (for a deficit with none, over the stores the model fills), floored to 32.32 per tick so it never rounds above, and booked as a source or sink at those stores while the stores it drains hold and those it fills have room (learned coupling, I6). A positive residual on a positive measured flow counts as a learned source (I9). |
| G2-9 | **Glass state is not paged.** The compile, its passed window and the replay's regime state live in memory only. After a restore, a district that was replaying is marked dirty and goes on by the black box (or freezes) from the next step, its stores' changes carried, until it is loaded and recompiled. No page format change in G2. Ben decided on 2026-10-21 to page it (For Ben 2 (a), G2-9 in DECISIONS.md), as the first follow-up after G2. |
| G2-10 | **Regime cache per part.** Solves are kept by regime key, `glass.regimeCache` (4) per part, least recently used out, so a controller cycling between two regimes solves each once. |
| G2-11 | **Partial-load cut (I7, G-B6).** Goods edges between a live and a simulated node are the cut. When goods cross from the live side, `PoolKey::Stock(d, g)` holds the live side's supply (what the live stores hold plus what the district owes) and the simulated side draws it there; its change from the start is owed. A cut good that runs dry stops the live node that would send it, until the supply is back at a holding threshold. The other way is capped at the live receivers' room (G2-66). A cut keeps the district one part. |
| G2-12 | **Time shift at a breakpoint.** The regime that just ended is shifted back in time until the pool that fired sits on its canonical carry (`shift_back`), so a breakpoint costs its regime under a tick and the next regime starts from a reproducible carry. A cycle's pieces record their fires and apply the same shift, so a closed-form period equals the stepped one. A pool nothing drains (an output store at 0) is not flagged empty, because no watch would restart its carry. |
| G2-13 | **Closed-form cycles (G-B9).** A regime sequence that repeats exactly (same flags, same watched levels, zero drift on every pool that decides a breakpoint) becomes one `Body::Cycle` over as many whole periods as provably change nothing else (128 marks kept, 4 candidate periods per mark, at most 2^40 ticks, 2 units of margin per pool). A controller loop costs one process per stretch, not two breakpoints per period. This is the periodic edge of §4.2 for clocks and controllers; trains, ships and packages are G3. |
| G2-14 | **Imports reach the machines.** An import (a train's unload, a give-back, an injection, a package) into a replaying district with no cut moves into the covered store that feeds the graph with that good (one with room first, then the lowest inventory index), so the machines see it; the store's change is written back once at the reconcile. A good no covered store feeds, and every import across a cut, waits in `Stock` for the reconcile. |
| G2-15 | **An edit freezes.** An IND_EDIT on a replaying district stops the glass replay (what it did is carried) and the district freezes until the recompile passes a window. |
| G2-16 | **What keeps the black box in G2.** A district with deposits, cross-district links, a PowerGrid source, a sink, or damage replays by the black box, as does one whose glass replay fell back. |
| G2-17 | **Chunk ticking.** A Java side that sends no IND_CHUNK_TICKING is read as "every loaded chunk ticks". The engine reads that off an empty ticking set, so a district none of whose chunks ticks also reads its loaded chunks as running (For Ben 13). |
| G2-18 | **Moving frames.** A compile on a moving frame (a Sable ship, frame not 0) has no world positions: every node is simulated. |
| G2-19 | **Empty flags clear on standing stock.** A store flagged empty that holds stock while nothing arrives is a source again: a chest a funnel kept nearly empty is not a source while goods pass through it, but what is left drains once they stop. Not for a hub (G2-22): its other parts drain it, which this part cannot see. |
| G2-20 | **The switch flips a tick early.** Each test looks one tick ahead (a bound the process could not run a tick under is a crossing now), so the replay's threshold switch flips a tick before Create's. It flips a little more often than Create (by its band over the band less two) and makes a little less. |
| G2-21 | **A machine's cycle is continuous.** The replay books a consumer's output at its rate, not at the end of each cycle, so at any read it may be one item ahead of Create's (the item in the cycle). The tests allow that one item. |
| G2-22 | **Parts.** The graph splits by union-find into pieces that share nothing but stores they only draw from (hubs). Each part is its own process with its own regimes, cache and cycles, so two loops of unequal periods each find their cycle. A hub is in every part it feeds; a cycle's steepest drain is its safe envelope there, a hub's empty flags are sticky, and its last units go to whichever part's watch fires first. A cut or a residual keeps the district one part. |
| G2-23 | **A heater's burn is not latency.** A heater's rate is its burn per fuel, not a transit: a fuel lights it at once, so the latency map gives heaters 0. |
| G2-24 | **The first solve round starts with heaters lit.** The duty loop starts from the gated heaters' assumed duty, so a heater fed by the line it heats is not read as out for good on the first round. L only comes down from there. |
| G2-26 | **One slot per arriving good first.** A store's free slots go one to each arriving good with no part-filled stack (heaviest first while slots last), then the rest split by rate. By rate alone a rare byproduct's share floors to no slot and the store reads full at once. |
| G2-27 | **An unconverged solve falls back.** A solve that did not settle books flows that do not balance at its nodes (a loop whose only exit is full grows round by round): goods would leave the stores and arrive nowhere. The replay falls back as Unsettled instead. |
| G2-28 | **Rounding one tick ahead.** A rate's reach is ceil of its 32.32 net per tick in units (the most one tick moves); a controller threshold uses reach + 1. Every flag test compares a level against the reach, so no process runs a tick past a bound. |
| G2-57 | **A bounded good inside its window takes no residual.** GLASSBOX.md §5.3: inside [L, U] the bounded rules are calibrated to the measurement; only a measured production above U is something unmodelled producing, and its excess over U is the learned source. A residual on one bounded good alone booked it from nothing when its recipe partners' windows were narrower (the exit run's package_loop made 1,012 clay balls from no clay). Calibrating bounded flows inside the solve is not built: inside [L, U] the replay runs L. |
| G2-63 | **The glass compiler compiles and fingerprints only block-ticking chunks.** A chunk that is loaded but not ticking (a view-distance border, or the neighbour of a kept chunk) holds block entities that have not run their first tick: a generator has no kinetic network and a deployer no player. The fingerprint saw a change, and the compile from them was a different graph. In the native m6 exit run the partial boiler copy straddled a chunk corner. Its third chunk loaded FULL-only 86 ticks into the replay, compile 4 replaced the passed compile 3, and the district stayed not eligible (frozen) for the 5,900 ticks away. A compile now waits until every chunk of the district is block-ticking, and a fingerprint pass skips chunks that are not. |
| G2-66 | **The cut from the simulated side is capped at its live receivers' room (I7, G-B2).** For each good the simulated side sends across the cut, the replay may raise `Stock(d, g)` to its start level plus the room its live receivers had at the start: a live store's free slots (or millibuckets) for the good, from its declared capacity (`Setup.live_decls`, or the node's own capacity) and its contents; 0 for a live machine, mover or other node, which holds nothing a reload could place. A watch (`Watch::Seal`) and a settle step a tick ahead seal the good there. A sealed good gates the simulated node that sends it (when a simulated store is the sender, the live machine it feeds; a store to store edge marks the live store full) until the replay ends. A receiving store of unknown capacity leaves the good unbounded, as a covered store of unknown capacity is. Tests: `a_cut_into_a_live_store_carries_no_more_than_its_room` (312 sheets of room: 313 brass pressed, 312 sheets land in the live store, nothing owed) and `a_cut_into_a_live_mover_carries_nothing` (the press stays off). |
| G2-67 | **A glass replay's `Stock` change is planned with the district's owed.** The cut's carry both ways, and the imports that wait in `Stock` (G2-14), were planned first over the covered inventories, so sheets a simulated press carried into a live store landed in the covered brass store. They now join what the district owes: over every inventory in a full round, where the plan prefers an inventory that received or holds the good (the live receiver), and over the covered part in a partial round, as before. G2-11 always said the change is owed. |

## Belt side insertion and the over-fed loop (GB72, decided by Ben as recommended)

| Id | Choice |
|---|---|
| G2-29 | Through traffic is marked by a new edge flag, THROUGH = 16 (records.rs, GlassRecords.java, docs/industry-boundary.md), not inferred from edge kind or order. CreateCompile sets it on a belt's chain links and the taker split link; a belt's end edge is not THROUGH. |
| G2-30 | Side insertions at a belt segment share one proportional priority group (`inserted_i = s_i min(1, c / sum s)`); through traffic takes the remainder. From `BeltInventory.canInsertAtFromSide`: an insertion waits only for its own side's last insertion. |
| G2-31 | A merge is bounded (SHARED_CAPACITY) only when the segment binds with more than one input flowing; below capacity it is exact. |
| G2-32 | The merge rule applies only to belt movers with both a THROUGH in-edge and another in-edge. |
| G2-33 | The stall is decided once after L converges, then L is re-solved (up to another `solveRoundsMax` rounds). |
| G2-34 | The feed f is measured by a probe solve with the loop's nodes as sinks. |
| G2-35 | A loop stalls when its host is SEQUENCED, on a cycle and saturated, its goods loop holds no store, and f > mu for some base good; f = mu runs. |
| G2-36 | mu is recipe capacity times heat factor times base input amount at the converged domain level. |
| G2-37 | A stalled loop gets L = 0 on every goods edge whose head is in the loop (parts chutes and feed included); U is untouched and the class stays bounded (cycle). |
| G2-38 | Loops with P at most 2 passes are documented but not exempted: the stall applies for any f > mu (see For Ben). |
| G2-39 | Both rules sit behind pub consts (`BELT_INSERTIONS_FIRST`, `OVERFED_LOOPS_STALL`, both true), with `solve_with` to flip them per call. |
| G2-40 | The LP passes each good through a pass-through machine with no recipes (the loop's deployers), as L and the linear bound already did. |
| G2-41 | Corpus 6 is split into `PrecisionMechanismFactory.layout(h, feedRpm)` and `checkBelts(h)`, shared with the new `precision_overfed` factory (feeder at 64 RPM, warm-up 1000, window 2400). |
| G2-42 | `precision_overfed` expects EXACT 0 for sheet, cogwheel, large cogwheel, nugget, mechanism and andesite alloy. Its exit window is [0, U], so only its smoke test checks the jam itself. |
| G2-43 | GLASSBOX.md §2.3's "Belt insertion" row (1 block against 1.0625) is left as is, to keep the docs diff to §3.3 and §3.4. |

## The exit test harness (GameTests, `GlassReplayGameTests`)

| Id | Choice |
|---|---|
| G2-25 | The harness credits an input's work in progress (goods inside machines at the end) as yield within tolerance only, never toward "at or below the control"; the stub engine applies the offline reconcile as the native one does. |
| G2-55 | Each corpus factory runs as two copies, one kept loaded (the control) and one far away and unloaded (simulated). The far place is laid out as vanilla's `StructureUtils` lays out the yard (relative y -2..-1 stone, y 0 air, y 1 polished andesite, air above, vanilla's clear margin), so blocks that need support (a diode on the floor, a boiler's channel dug into it) stand as in the control. Each copy draws its own package frequency at build. While both are loaded, the harness samples both copies' declared stores every 100 ticks; if any good's change over the last 2400 ticks differs by more than one stack or batch in flight, plus 3 control sigma over the window, plus a tenth of the control's change, every row of that factory reads INVALID COPY and fails. |
| G2-56 | `package_loop` is marked KNOWN GAP in the table and is judged on the at or below half only: a within miss prints "KNOWN GAP (package addresses not modelled, For Ben 1)" and does not fail the test, so the create suite stays green on main for every session's landing gate. Above the control, or an INVALID COPY, still fails it. The exit test is not met for `package_loop` until package routing by address is modelled (G3). |
| G2-64 | **A dead far copy is built again once.** Once one window (2400 ticks) of samples is in, the copy check's window test runs from the first sample (lenient: the control's current sigma and q, the in-flight term uncapped). On any good off, the harness logs "far copy dead at warm-up, rebuilt" with both copies' kinetics, builds both copies again and resets the samples; dead a second time, it fails as INVALID COPY. Stub runs m5 (press_sheets) and m6 (precision_mechanisms) each had a far copy whose belts never started; the cause was the far chunks' entities not yet loaded (G2-68). |
| G2-65 | **Fixed partial placement and a straddle smoke.** The partial rows put a chunk border between the stores on the main axis and centre the control's whole build footprint inside one chunk on the other, so the far district is 2 chunks, one ticking and one FULL-only (replayed, since the other ticks). `straddle_boiler_overstress` puts a border through the footprint on both axes (3 or more chunks, a corner) and passes only if every far report read while away carries the first far report's compile number and none reads other than GLASS after the first GLASS one; its rows print as information and are never part of the verdict. |
| G2-68 | **The far copy is built only once its chunks tick block entities.** `Districts.blockTicking` reads the ticket alone, and a chunk forced a tick ago passes it while its entities are still being read on the entity IOWorker. Vanilla ticks no block entity of a chunk whose entities are not loaded (`LevelChunk.isTicking`) and runs no scheduled tick before its neighbours are FULL (`isPositionTickingWithEntitiesLoaded`). Usually the entities load 20 to 60 ticks after the force (60 to 350 ms), and the copy starts a little late. On a loaded server they can take 4,000 ticks or more (about 5 s on the unthrottled GameTest server). Then the copy is built into chunks where nothing runs, every kinetic block entity stays unticked (`needsSpeedUpdate`), and the copy stands dead through the warm-up window. A temporary diagnostic run (stub) showed this: threshold_smelter and boiler_overstress had 9 and 8 of 9 far chunks with entities not loaded at test tick 4,000, while the yard is always ready, because the GameTest framework waits for it to be entity-ticking. The other dead copies match it: partial boiler_overstress had 31 kinetic, all still and none on a network; press_sheets' far compile stayed at 6 nodes and 3 edges while the control's went to 7 and 5. The harness now waits on `notTicking` before it builds (and before it rebuilds after a dead warm-up) for every far chunk and every yard chunk: loaded, block-ticking by its ticket, inside the world border, entities loaded, neighbours FULL, entity-ticking, and not held by the tick gate. Each failed check sleeps 5 ms. The run fails after `IO_TICKS` (12,000) ticks and logs `far chunks = N chunks tick after T ticks and M ms (first waited on ...)`. A dead warm-up's kinetics line says why a source's chunk does not tick. Test: `aChunkWithItsEntitiesUnloadedTicksNoBlockEntity` holds the entity IOWorker with a foreground task and checks a shaft in a forced chunk: block-ticking with its neighbours FULL, not ticking, and `notTicking` says why; once the IO goes, the shaft ticks. Checked on fresh worlds: the three harness tests fail on the harness of 8ff2a8e and pass after the fix; then stub 169 of 169 and native 169 of 169, with no rebuild. In that stub run, saw_shafts waited 907 ticks (5,973 ms) on a far chunk whose entities were not loaded; native's longest wait was 338 ticks (2,140 ms). |
| G2-69 | **The reload waits on the same readiness.** "Reloaded" (`loadedAt`, the end of the latency's load gap) is now the tick every far chunk ticks block entities again, not the tick it was block-ticking by its ticket, so the load gap counts the ticks the far copy did not run. This includes up to 20 tick-gate ticks while the reconcile lands. Both waits now time out after `IO_TICKS` with a failure instead of waiting forever. |
| G2-70 | **The warm-up check catches a far copy that did not move at all.** It is also dead when none of its goods' changes since the first sample is non-zero while the control moved at least a stack of some good. Native d20e6bd's press_sheets passed the tolerance test with nothing moved: its 124 in flight (a press's batch) came within 126 sheets and 128 ingots. The rule is per copy, not per good, so a copy that lags in one good is still judged by the tolerance alone. The other verdict rules and the copy check at the snapshot are unchanged. `warmVerdict` holds the rule. Test: `theWarmUpCatchesACopyThatNeverMoved` covers press_sheets' numbers, a lagging copy, a control under a stack and a still control. |
| G2-71 | **The warm-up check runs on the unjudged stub too.** It runs with one stack in flight and no noise term (no report without natives), as the copy check has there. Stub d20e6bd's brass_tunnels went its whole run with a dead far copy and no warm-up line. |
| G2-72 | **A brass diode's `powering` is volatile in the Create fingerprint.** A pulse timer's POWERING is on for 2 ticks of every period: the clock's state, not the build. With it hashed, a fingerprint pass that lands in a pulse mismatches the compile. Passes run every 20 ticks and the pulse is 2 ticks in 40, so when the pulse holds a multiple of 20 (about 1 in 10 builds), every second pass marks the district dirty. That recompile storm is native d20e6bd's `glassexit.package_loop`: a recompile every 20 ticks up to compile 1020, "revision 1 (district none)", that is, no sweep between compiles. It is For Ben 10's churn, and it is not related to the dead far copies. Test: `aPulseTimersPulseIsNotStructure` checks one fingerprint over a 40-tick period with the pulse seen. |
| G2-78 | **The held-chunk test's IO hold lets go only when the test does.** `aChunkWithItsEntitiesUnloadedTicksNoBlockEntity` failed once on native d3b4e5a: "the held chunk's entities are not loaded" within the 40 held ticks, although its entities were not loaded at the hold. The blocker on the entity IOWorker waited at most 30 s, and the chunk took longer than that to be ready: 44 s from the batch start in that run, against 17 to 28 s in the five runs where the hold held (stub and native on ec92b8b and on feat/glassbox-g2-copy, and the pre-fix check of 8ff2a8e). Nearly all of that time is the force itself: `setChunkForced` generates the chunk synchronously (`getChunk`), and its entity read is queued only when it reaches FULL at the end of that call. A confirmation run on the stub with a 5 s backstop logged "forced in 29653 ms, ready 100 ms later; the hold let go: the backstop, 5 s", with entities not loaded at the hold point and the test failing as a hold that let go. So once the 30 s blocker gave up during the force, the read queued at FULL ran unblocked and the entities landed within the held ticks. The hold is now an `IoHold`: the blocker lets go when the test releases it, when the test is done, or when the server stops (it polls every 100 ms; `IOWorker.close` waits on its mailbox). It keeps a 600 s backstop only. Each held tick first checks that the hold still holds, so a hold that let go fails as "the entity IO hold holds (let go: ...)". It does not fail as missing entities. A load while the hold holds fails as one that skipped the entity IOWorker. Before it forces the chunk, the test checks that the chunk is not loaded yet and that `EntityStorage.emptyChunks` does not hold it (a known-empty chunk's load never queues on the IOWorker). The hold line logs how long the force and the wait took, and the hold's state. I chose to make the hold reliable rather than skip a leaked hold: a leak now means a load path the harness does not know, which is worth a failure. |

## Fan currents and districts (the crushing_wash_blast bug)

| Id | Choice |
|---|---|
| G2-73 | **A fan's air current links districts.** This was the crushing_wash_blast bug (Known bugs). When the fans (structure x14) started a chunk column, their chunk was never a member. Detection grows from seeds through block entities that touch across a chunk border and through plugin links. The harness seeds the stores and the anchor, all in the belts' chunk, and at x13 to x14 there is only water or lava, no block entity. So the compile never visited the fans, and their 14 zones were missing: 12 nodes and 10 edges. Every log matches. The three runs with x13 at chunk-local 15 (native 86b1369, stub m5, stub d20e6bd) compiled 12 nodes, and every other alignment compiled 26. The old note's z split was that run's z alignment, not the cause. In play a placed fan is a seed and joins by adjacency, but two cases left its zones out the same way: a fan nobody placed (a schematic, a base built before mic_rtsim) and a current that crosses a whole chunk. `CreatePlugin.links` (`FanLinks`) now links a fan to the belt segments and depots in other chunks that its current reaches, at its height or one below. It also links a host to the horizontal fans in other chunks whose current reaches it. For that it casts up to 20 blocks along each horizontal ray from the host and from the block above it. A ray ends at the first full block that is not fan-transparent, and the fan found is checked with its own `AirCurrent.getFlowLimit`. Neither side loads a chunk. A blaze burner in the current is not linked, because the plugin does not count burners industrial. |
| G2-74 | **The link's reach is the current at full speed, cut by the blocks.** It is the larger of `fanPushDistance` and `fanPullDistance` (20 each by default) through `getFlowLimit`, not the reach at the fan's speed now. Speed is state (GB51), and a clutch or a stopped motor would otherwise move chunks in and out of the district. A slow fan therefore links hosts its current does not reach now; the compile still builds its zones from the walked speed. |
| G2-75 | **A fan's current across a district boundary is a conveyor crossing.** For a fan and a host on its current line within the full-speed reach, either way round, `CreatePlugin.crossing` returns CONVEYOR_CROSSES (7), tested before rotation. A fan and a belt are both kinetic, so before this such a pair said ROTATION_CROSSES, and a fan and a depot said nothing. It matters only where a split pins them apart or an exclusion keeps one out. |
| G2-76 | **The tests, and what stays out of the district.** `GlassFanLinkGameTests.aFanThatStartsAChunkIsInItsBeltsDistrict` builds crushing_wash_blast in the yard through the far helper. It is shifted so the fans are at chunk-local x 0 and the build sits in one chunk row, and seeded as the harness seeds it. It checks that the anchor's district holds the fans' chunk and that the compile has 14 fan zones, 26 nodes and 24 edges. `aFansCurrentAcrossAChunkJoinsItsDepot` and `aDepotFindsTheFanThatBlowsAtIt` put a depot at chunk-local x 15 and a washing fan 17 blocks east, at local 0 of the chunk after next, and seed one side each. Before the fix all three failed (stub at 48a58c8, the static tests alone): the anchor's district was the belts' chunk alone, and the depot's or the fan's chunk, whichever was not seeded, was in no district. ## Bounding the solve (OmegaSrv rehearsal finding 5, DR9)

The rehearsal's main base (1,258 nodes, 1,790 edges) ran every sampled solve to its 278-round cap, 2.4 to 5.4 s inside the engine's step, and a stop during one could not save the pages (docs/deploy-rehearsal-omegasrv.md 6.7). The regression case is `base_shaped` in `glass/tests.rs`: N cells, each a store feeding a funnel, a belt and a press on a belt loop, over-fed (one cell at a feed of 9 a tick settles in 8,614 rounds). The world itself was not used; no graph dump of the real base exists.

| Id | Choice |
|---|---|
| G2-58 | **A work budget for the rounds (`glass.solveWorkMax`, 200,000).** Each round costs a count fixed by the graph (`Solver::round_work`: per edge one; per node 1 plus its slots in and out, plus per slot arriving its out-edges and its recipes' terms, plus its terms per out-edge). Before a round the solve checks the round fits in what is left; if not it stops, unconverged and `starved`, as at `solveRoundsMax`. The first round of a top-level solve always runs. Over-fed loop probes (GB72) draw on the same budget; a probe that runs out leaves its stall undecided and the solve unconverged. No clock, so a journal replay stops exactly where the live run did. Measured on the laptop: 20 to 60 ns a unit at a load of 12 to 17 (the base fixture), up to 170 under heavy load. |
| G2-59 | **A work budget for the LPs (`glass.lpWorkMax`, 20,000,000).** An LP costs its variables squared plus a fixed set-up (G2-62). A good whose LPs do not fit keeps the linear bound for U, which is valid and looser. |
| G2-60 | **Unconverged parts are bounded with L = 0.** An unconverged solve's last round can sit above the fixed point (an over-fed loop carried 0.111 a tick into a press that settles at 0.1), so its flows are not a lower bound. The parts still moving are joined through recipes, cycles and every kinetic domain. A part is still moving when, in the last round, any node in it had a warming back edge, a falling override, a domain level change, a duty change or a back-edge acceptance change; also when a cyclic SEQUENCED machine's stall was never decided. Those parts become BOUNDED (why UNCONVERGED) with L = 0 on every goods edge into them. Parts that settled keep their exact flows: a line beside a still-moving loop stays EXACT. The replay still falls back on any unconverged solve (G2-27). |
| G2-61 | **A replay regime change runs at most `buildSolvesMax` (16) fresh solves** past the regime cache while its flags and holds settle; past them the district falls back as Unsettled. The unit tests need at most 4. |
| G2-62 | **What the budgets do not count is made linear.** Reachability and slots go word by word over the goods a node can hold, not over every good: a 3,200-node, 800-good base took 12.4 ms in `Solver::new` before and 0.3 to 0.46 ms after. The LPs' cones and free objectives start from a per-good index of read-store edges built once, not a scan of the graph per good. Each LP costs `LP_WORK_FIXED` (50,000) plus its variables squared, because a 6-variable LP takes about 25 us and its squared variables account for 20 ns. A good's parts are costed together before any runs, so a good that does not fit spends nothing. Each cone walked costs its nodes, so once the work is spent no good walks one. `glass_table` at 10,000 nodes: 116 ms (no slack) and 339 ms (slack) before, 12 to 45 ms after (its 1,000-part LP no longer fits, so its U is the linear bound). |

Measured (`base_solve_cpu`, ignored; nice 10, budget and cap only side by side, 5 runs each, medians):

| Base | Budget | Cap only (as before) |
|---|---|---|
| 10 cells (80 nodes) | 1.0 to 2.5 ms, 22 rounds, converged | the same |
| 138 cells (1,104 nodes) | 15.5 ms (load 12), 41 ms (load 38), 32 rounds | 80 ms (load 12), 203 ms (load 38), 278 rounds |
| 400 cells (3,200 nodes) | 25.7 ms (load 12), 36 ms (load 38), 11 rounds | 657 ms (load 12), 1,622 ms (load 38), 802 rounds |

Rounds alone, before G2-62, at a load of 32 to 52: 138 cells 27 to 31 ms against 133 to 152 ms; 400 cells 81 to 115 ms against 1,035 to 1,048 ms.

Not built: spreading the remaining rounds over later steps. The solver's state is in memory and not paged, so a solve carried across steps would not replay the same after a restart.
