Today: guns are piles of ingots clicked together at the Gun Smith Table

  • The AKM (tacz:ak47) costs 2,394 items, 2,048 of them birch logs.
  • The M107 costs 320 steel ingots.
  • Every one of the 54 guns eats a Create Big Cannons autocannon barrel, breech and recoil spring, so a 9 mm pistol barrel is literally a cannon block.
  • Five guns need boss drops, and 347 precision mechanisms are spread across the gun list.
  • Ammo is one click: 1 smokeless powder, 1 brass, 1 copper, 1 lead and 1 fulminated mercury give 18 to 100 rounds.

After: the player builds an arms industry

  • Steel goes into bar stock, and bar stock goes down a barrel line (saw, harden, gun-drill, rifle, proof).
  • Receivers are milled from forgings, or stamped from sheet. Actions are machined and heat-treated.
  • An ammunition plant draws brass cases, swages bullets, charges primers and loads them in measured lots with propellant from Destroy.
  • A gun is assembled from 4 to 9 manufactured items it actually has.
  • Parts carry how well they were made. A barrel cut with a fresh broach makes a more accurate gun. An unproofed barrel that was water-quenched can burst in the field.

What gets built

  • One new Java mod, mic_machining: four tool heads that sit over a belt or depot like a Mechanical Press, an oil-quench fan process, and a small set of item data for build quality.
  • A KubeJS layer for the parts and all recipes.

The heads are generic: the same Die Ram that draws a 9 mm case also draws CBC cartridges, stamps magazine bodies, rivets RHA plate and loads nuclear fuel rods. What you decide: eight questions, each with a recommended answer.

2Design rules

  1. No piles. Every gun recipe is 4 to 9 manufactured items (so it fits a 3×3 crafter). A strong gun costs more because its parts need better tools and more processes, not because it needs 300 ingots.
  2. The process is the progression. Each tier is gated by a capability the player builds: a harder cutter, a die, a heat-treatment route, a propellant. Tiers are not gated by an arbitrary rare ingredient. The select-fire sear needs an HSS cutter, and HSS needs tungsten, so the tungsten age is still where automatic weapons arrive; it simply arrives for a real reason.
  3. One idiom, many products. A tool head sits over a belt or depot exactly like Create's Mechanical Press and takes a tool. The tool chooses the operation (drill, broach, die, gauge), and the head's filter slot chooses the output when one tool can make several things, as Create's saw does for stonecutting. Every head also works as a step inside sequenced assembly.
  4. Consequences, not refusals. Nothing is blocked for being badly made:
    • A worn tool makes rough parts; rough parts make a less accurate gun.
    • Water quenching alloy steel risks a hidden crack. Proof firing finds it; skipping proof can cost you the gun.
  5. Hand first, factory later. Every part has a hand route (Overgeared forge, TaCZ tables) and a factory route. The factory is faster and makes better parts. It is the reward, never the requirement.
  6. Real numbers. Charges, case masses, cutting speeds and tool-life exponents come from real firearms practice and machining tables. Balance knobs are marked TUNE.

3The machine shop: four tool heads

All four are new blocks in mic_machining. They share one base: Create's public PressingBehaviour driven by our own PressingBehaviourSpecifics. Vintage Improvements' Curving Press is built the same way. The base gives every head the following, for free:

  • Placement: it works over a belt, a depot or a basin, and as a sequenced-assembly step.
  • Tool slot: right-click a tool in, or feed it from a funnel or Mechanical Arm on the back face. That back-face feed is an automatic tool changer: when a tool breaks, the next one is pulled in.
  • Filter slot: chooses the output when several recipes share the same input and tool.
  • Goggle readout and Display Source: tool name, wear percentage, rated RPM and current RPM, with a red overspeed warning. A Display Link can put "Broach: 37 % left" on a factory board.
  • Comparator output: proportional to tool wear.

3.1 Spindle Head (machining)

What it does: drills, mills, broaches (rifling and keyways) and hobs gears on the item below it. Cutters (12 items) are 4 shapes in 3 steels: mic_machining:<steel>_<shape>, where shape is gun_drill, end_mill, broach or gear_hob.

SteelMade fromRated speedTaylor exponent nTier
Carbon tool steelsteel carburised with coal (Metallurgy alloying), cast as a tool blank32 RPM0.1G1
High-speed steel (HSS)steel + tungsten + chromium (Metallurgy alloying, HYPERHEATED), the T1 recipe without vanadium (the pack has none)64 RPM0.125G3
Cemented carbidetungsten + graphite in Destroy's Arc Furnace gives tungsten carbide; pressed with nickel and sintered256 RPM0.25G5

Why those speeds. Handbook cutting speeds for steel are roughly 12 m/min for carbon tool steel, 27 m/min for HSS and 110 m/min for carbide, a ratio near 1 : 2 : 9. Create tops out at 256 RPM, so carbide is pinned to 256 and the others keep the ratio (1 : 2 : 8).

Overspeed. Above rated speed, each operation costs this many wear points. That is Taylor's tool-life law (V·T^n = C), so the penalty is real and steep:

(RPM / rated)^(1/n)
  • An HSS cutter at twice its rated speed wears 2^8 = 256 times faster and dies in a few parts.
  • Carbide at twice rated wears 16 times faster.

The goggles show the multiplier, so the rule the player learns is simple: run each cutter at its rated speed.

Durability at rated speed: carbon 64, HSS 256, carbide 1,024 operations TUNE.

Sharpening. A blunt carbon or HSS cutter is reground on Metallurgy's Mechanical Belt Grinder (a grinding recipe that returns it at full durability). Carbide cannot be reground on sandpaper; scrap carbide is melted back to tungsten.

Grade. The grade of a part is set by the cutter at the part's last cut. The thresholds are TUNE.

Cutter condition at the cutPart grade
above 2/3 durability and not over speedPrecision
1/3 to 2/3Standard
below 1/3, or cut over speedRough

Making the first cutters. The first carbon cutters are hand-forged on the Overgeared anvil, hardened by hand (heat treatment) and ground on the belt grinder. After that the Spindle makes its own cutters from tool-steel blanks, as real shops do.

3.2 Die Ram (die pressing and batch pressing)

What it does:

  • Over a belt or depot: blanking, cupping, deep drawing, stamping, swaging, riveting and coining.
  • Over a basin: multi-ingredient batch pressing with counts, which is how ammunition is loaded (the ammunition plant).

This is the same pattern as Create's own press, where belt means pressing and basin means compacting.

Dies. There is one item, mic_machining:die, carrying a die_profile component. JEI shows each profile as its own stack.

  • Making dies: a tool-steel blank goes under a Spindle with an end mill, the Spindle's filter picks the profile, and the die is then hardened.
  • Wear: each stroke costs 1 point, and a die lasts 512 strokes TUNE.
  • Grade: dies wear but do not grade parts. Drawn and stamped parts come out Standard.

3.4 Induction Head (induction hardening, tempering, annealing)

What it does: heats the part below it with Power Grid electricity, then sprays it from its own tank. This is real induction hardening, which is how gun parts, shafts and gears are hardened in industry. Modes, set by wrench-scroll:

ModeQuenchResult
Hardenyesthe heat_treatment component becomes hardened
Tempernotempered
Annealnosoftens drawn brass between draws

Heat time from physics:

ticks = 20 · m · c · ΔT / P
  • m is the part's mass (from a data table), c = 490 J/kg·K for steel, and ΔT = 825 K (room temperature to 845 °C, the austenitizing temperature of 4140, the usual barrel and receiver steel).
  • P is the electrical power the head draws.
  • Worked example, a 1.2 kg rifle barrel blank: 1.2 × 490 × 825 = 485 kJ. At 10 kW that is 48.5 s (970 ticks). A 10 g spring takes 0.4 s.
  • VERIFY what wattages Power Grid generators really deliver. A config multiplier may be needed so the times feel right.

Quench medium matters (heat treatment). Why have it when fans exist: it is one block instead of a three-fan line, it is precise, and it is the pack's first real reason to run Power Grid power into a factory floor.

4.1 Heat treatment, three routes

Heat-treatment state is a component on the part, mic_machining:heat_treatment = hardened or tempered, not a separate item for every state. A recipe that needs a hardened part asks for the component with NeoForge's neoforge:components ingredient. TaCZ's table, Create and Metallurgy all accept NeoForge custom ingredients (TaCZ registers its own tacz:nbt ingredient the same way).

The oil-quench fan type is new: mic_machining:oil_quenching, priority 450.

  • Catalyst: any fluid in #mic_machining:quench_oils. That is Create Diesel Generators' plant oil now, and mic_petrochem's mineral oil when it ships. Canola-type vegetable oil is a real industrial quenchant.
  • Template: Create Dragons Plus's fluid-keyed "ending" fan type, the same registry and the same isValidAt pattern. Air passes through fluid catalysts, as it does through lava for blasting.

Fan traps found, and how the plan avoids them:

  • Blasting burns anything without a smelting recipe. BlastingType.canProcess is true for any non-fireproof item, and process then returns nothing. So every heat-treatable part gets an explicit blasting recipe into the hot workpiece, and the hot workpiece carries minecraft:fire_resistant so it is not burned on the next pass.
  • CBC's built-up cannon heating checks the blasting type by identity. We never wrap or replace blasting; the new type has its own catalyst.

Water versus oil is a consequence, not a refusal.

  • Plain carbon steel parts (G1 to G2) may be water-quenched freely.
  • Alloy parts (HSS, stainless, spring steel, and anything tagged #mic_machining:alloy_parts) come out of water with the risk component quench_crack, shown on the tooltip as "Water-quenched: crack risk".
  • The risk is resolved, not rolled, at the first proof or inspection, or at final assembly. The chance is TUNE, starting at 10 %.
  • Oil quench carries no risk.
  • This matches real practice: 4140 and spring steels are oil-quenched because water cracks them.

The fan recipes (a blasting recipe into the hot workpiece, then splashing, oil and smoking out of it) are generated by one KubeJS loop from a table of heat-treatable items, so a new part needs one table line.

4.3 Polymer parts (Metallurgy foundry and casting)

Real process: glass-filled nylon is injection-moulded into frames and furniture; PMMA into lenses. In game (no new machine):

  1. Destroy's nylon, PVC or PMMA items melt in a Metallurgy foundry basin (heated). Melting recipes accept any item to any fluid.
  2. The melt goes to three new KubeJS fluids: kubejs:molten_nylon, kubejs:molten_pvc and kubejs:molten_pmma.
  3. It is cast on Metallurgy's casting table into a steel mould. Casting takes any fluid plus any ingredient as the mould, and mold_consumed: false keeps it.

Moulds: one item, mic_machining:mould, with a profile: polymer_frame, polymer_furniture, grip, lens, magazine_body_polymer. A mould is a steel block milled by the Spindle, with its filter choosing the profile.

7The ammunition plant

7.1 The numbers and where they come from

Three anchors, chosen once, turn real masses into recipe counts:

16 g
1 powder item: the charge for one 50-round lot of 9 mm at 0.32 g (5 grains) per round. It applies to minecraft:gunpowder, kubejs:smokeless_powder and destroy:cordite_rods alike.
320 g
1 brass, copper or lead sheet or ingot
Retail box
the lot size: pistol 50, rifle 20, magnum pistol 20, .338 and .50 BMG 10, 12 gauge 25

From these:

  • Powder per lot = round(lot × charge ÷ 16 g), at least 1.
  • Black powder fills the case, as it did historically: charge = the case's water capacity × 0.9 g/cm³ bulk density. This reproduces the real .45-70 (70 gr, 4.5 g) and the 12-gauge 3-dram load (5.3 g).
  • Black-powder loads exist only for pistol, revolver and shotgun calibers plus .45-70 (your 2026-09-17 decision, kept).

8.2 Final assembly, two routes, same parts

  • Hand: the TaCZ Gun Smith Table (tacz:gun_smith_table_crafting, the parts below, ≤ 9 materials).
    • TaCZ fires no event when a gun is crafted. GunSmithTableMenu#doCraft extracts the stacks and spawns a static copy of the result.
    • So mic_machining adds one mixin there, loaded only when TaCZ is present. It reads the consumed parts' components and writes them onto the gun VERIFY against the unofficial port.
  • Line: a Create Mechanical Crafter running mic_machining:assembly.
    • That is a shaped crafting recipe whose assemble() merges the specified ingredients' components onto the result.
    • It works in a crafting table too.
    • The result is tacz:modern_kinetic_gun with custom_data {GunId, GunFireMode} (shaped results carry components; TaCZ's own ammo box recipe already does this).

9Walkthroughs

9.1 G1, by hand: your first shotgun (M870)

  1. Smelt steel as usual and build an Overgeared smithing anvil.
  2. Barrel blank.
    • Heat a steel ingot in a furnace until it glows (Overgeared heated).
    • On the anvil, choose the barrel-blank pattern and play the hammering minigame.
    • The blank remembers your result: PERFECT means Precision, POOR means a forging-defect risk.
  3. Harden it.
    • Reheat the blank and drop it in a water cauldron: it is hardened.
    • Put it in a smoker: it is tempered.
    • The tooltip shows "Tempered".
  4. Spindle Head.
    • Craft one from an andesite casing, a shaft, a cogwheel and 2 steel ingots.
    • Place it 2 blocks above a depot, and power it at 32 RPM (goggles: "Carbon gun drill, rated 32 RPM").
    • Put in a carbon gun drill (Overgeared-forged, hardened, ground on the belt grinder).
    • Drop the blank on the depot. It is bored, then bored again (ream) → smoothbore_barrel.
  5. Proof it.
    • Build a proof chamber instrument and an Inspection Head, then load one proof shell (a 12-gauge shell loaded with double powder).
    • The barrel is stamped "Proofed". If you got a forging defect, it bursts here and not in your hands.
  6. Receiver and action.
    • Hand-forge a receiver forging and mill it under the Spindle. Filter: Milled Receiver. Use an end mill.
    • Mill a bolt; harden and temper it.
    • Coil two springs on Vintage's coiling machine and temper them.
    • Craft the manual_action.
  7. Furniture. Saw logs, then turn the stock on the Vintage Lathe → wood_furniture.
  8. Gun Smith Table. M870 = milled receiver + manual action + smoothbore barrel + wood furniture. The tooltip shows "Barrel: Precision, Proofed · Made by Ben".
  9. Shells.
    • Die Ram over a depot with a cup die: brass sheet → 64 cups. Paper + cups → hulls.
    • At the Ammo Table: 25 hulls + 25 lead nuggets + 25 primers + 8 gunpowder → 25 black-powder 12-gauge shells.

9.2 G3, automated: an AKM line

The AKM today costs 2,394 items. Here it is five parts, each from a line. It is built at G3 and shoots once a G4 plant like 9.3 runs, with a load/762x39 die and bullet_30 instead of the 5.56 ones. The barrel line runs on one belt:

  1. Barrel line, on one belt:
    • Mechanical Saw (filter: barrel_blank)
    • blasting fan, then oil-quench fan (plant oil from CDG), then smoking fan, giving a tempered blank
    • Spindle (HSS gun drill, 64 RPM), then Spindle (HSS gun drill, ream), then Spindle (HSS broach), giving a rifle_barrel graded by the broach's condition
    • Inspection Head (gauge set: rough barrels are scrapped back to the foundry)
    • Inspection Head (proof chamber)
    • A Display Link shows the broach's wear on a board, and a Mechanical Arm swaps in a fresh broach from a chest when the comparator reads low.
  2. Receiver: Die Ram stamp/receiver on 2 steel sheets; Die Ram rivet makes rivets from rod; deployers fit them; the press closes them → stamped_receiver.
  3. Action:
    • Sequenced assembly on a manual_action: deploy spring → deploy precision mechanism → press → deploy HSS-milled sear → deploy spring → press → select_fire_action.
    • The sear and bolt were oil-quenched on the same fan line.
  4. Magazine: Die Ram stamp/magazine_body → deploy spring → deploy follower → box_magazine.
  5. Furniture: the Lathe turns wood_furniture.
  6. Assembly:
    • A 3×3 Mechanical Crafter running mic_machining:assembly: receiver, action, barrel, magazine, furniture → AKM, carrying the barrel's grade and proof stamp.
    • A Packager boxes it, and the Stock Keeper lists "AKM (Precision, Proofed) ×12".

9.3 G4, a 5.56 plant feeding the AKM's NATO cousins

  1. Cases:
    • Brass sheet → Die Ram cup → cups.
    • Induction Head (Anneal) → Die Ram draw/intermediate → intermediate_case, 49 per sheet.
  2. Bullets:
    • Copper sheet → Die Ram cup → jackets.
    • Then a basin under a Die Ram swage/22: 1 lead ingot + 64 jackets → 64 bullet_22.
  3. Primers:
    • Die Ram cup → primer cups.
    • Destroy's fulminated mercury + sulfur + glass powder, mixed → priming compound.
    • Basin under a Die Ram load/primer: 64 cups + 1 compound → 32 rifle primers (2 cups each).
  4. Powder: Destroy nitration gives nitrocellulose, and the existing heated mix gives kubejs:smokeless_powder.
  5. Loading:
    • Basin under a Die Ram with load/556x45: 20 cases + 20 bullets + 20 rifle primers + 2 smokeless → 20 rounds of 5.56.
    • A funnel feeds a Packager, which feeds the Stock Keeper.
