Nothing pops in front of you

An arrival is placed when its site loads with no player within 128 blocks. Chunks load past 128 blocks, so the body is there before you could see it. If you are near when it is due, it walks or flies in from its source's direction instead, so you watch a dragon arrive over the horizon or settlers walk in from the edge of the map. A site under a chunk loader, which never unloads, gets its arrival when the wait ends, once nobody is within 128 blocks. Nothing new enters the world: every body, structure and item is the pack's.

Farming slows itself: one or two bosses a year from a site you camp

Each site keeps one number, its pressure. Every member a player kills or takes adds 1 divided by the site's size: 1 for a den or a lair, a seventh for one warrior of a seven-warrior grove. Pressure halves every game year. When the site is short, the next member comes after the site's base wait doubled once for each unit of pressure, up to a cap. Deaths no player caused add nothing, so a world nobody visits never slows its own renewal.

What counts as your kill

  • A death vanilla credits to you: your hit in the last 5 seconds, or your pet's kill (a tamed dragon, a wolf).
  • Taking it: taming, a lead, Carry On, a capture item. It leaves the sim (A10).
  • For bosses (dragons, Frostmaw, the Umvuthi, Death Worms), any death no wild mob caused: your trap, a contraption, a turret, lava.
  • A home body missing at a census while rtsim ran. Luring a Frostmaw out of its den costs the den what killing it does, and if the stray dies later, that counts too.

Zombie sieges, raids, a dragon eating a herd and a camp losing a feud raise nothing. Today the dragon bridge sees only the killer, so a kill by your tamed dragon or your trap would never slow a lair; that changes.

Herds settle at about 60% of their range, and no dragon can finish one off

Today herds boom and crash. A 41-year run swings between 40,000 and 117,000 animals while one herd takes over and others die out: 4 to 15 ranges a world in 60 years, even with no player, and the dead stay dead. How many animals there are is set by the forage tuning.

  • Births slow as a herd fills its range (a Beverton-Holt term, one multiply per herd per year). A herd below its range's capacity grows, above it shrinks, and never overshoots.
  • Dragons hunt what is common and stop at a refuge of a tenth of the range's capacity, at least 8 animals: the animals in rough ground. A dragon whose hunting ground is eaten out leaves, and a newcomer settles only where there is food. Near villages, livestock keeps dragons fed.
  • Each spring, full herds send young to thin ones: 5% of the adults of herds above 40% of capacity walk to neighbouring ranges below 25%.
  • An empty range is refounded by a small herd from beyond the explored edge (12 animals, or 5% of capacity if larger), on average once in four years.

Factions fight only when it pays, so a fortress sees scouts, not raids

Replaced. The gate below is the design you rejected in EQ-5. The lifelike faction conflict page replaces it: each camp decides through a council with moods, wants, memories and wrong guesses, and 5 questions there wait for you. This section stays as the gate was proposed.

Today a goblin, bandit or raider camp climbs a grievance ladder (envoy, scouts, raid, war) with no check that its party can win. In the model a fortress faces 13.8 wars a year and kills 192 goblins a year, and a player who answers each loss with more wall starts an arms race that never ends.

The gate: before a war party goes out, the faction weighs the loot it expects to carry home against the warriors it expects to lose, using what it believes of your defence. It learns that from its scouts and its fights, and forgets over a game year. It asks terms first, half way between what a war is worth to it and what a war would cost you. Refuse and it attacks three days later. If no war is worth it, it sends scouts every 48 days. Camps weigh each other the same way.

A reset or an uninstall never mints a boss

Today deleting world/rtsim/ makes every empty lair "found with no dragon", and each gets one 20 days later: resetting is a way to farm bosses.

  • A site ledger in the world save (EQ-3): one row per site in mic_rtsim_sites.dat, beside the existing village file and outside world/rtsim/, so a reset keeps it. About 32 bytes a site plus 16 per tracked boss body; 5,000 sites is under 400 KB. It grows with exploration, never with time.
  • A farmed site stays farmed. The ledger keeps the pressure and the due time in world game time, which neither a reset nor /time set touches, so a reinstall cannot shorten a wait. A site found empty with no row waits the full cap, 192 days: lost history counts as farmed, never fresh.
  • Roosting dragons are ordinary saved mobs (EQ-2), puppets only while away, as the Mowzie plan already does for dens and groves. An uninstall leaves them in their lairs, and a reset finds them by census.
  • Missing is not dead. A boss body not found at a census stays on the ledger by its UUID. If it turns up while its site is still short, it is home and the replacement is cancelled; after a refill it is a stray, and its death counts against its old site.

What an uninstall can still do, as your rule that rtsim can come and go allows: a member that died away while its home was unloaded still has its saved body at home, so a player who uninstalls before that home loads can kill it once more for its loot. Nothing else.

Decided without asking you: 28 choices, each can be undone

The rule

  • Only losses a player caused raise pressure, so an unattended world stays full.
  • Pressure halves every 96 days (one game year), per site. This is the knob for farmed yield.
  • A player's loss is vanilla's kill credit (his hit in the last 5 seconds, his pet), every taking, and for bosses any death no wild mob caused.
  • A home body missing while rtsim ran is a player's loss; it stays tracked, and a stray's death counts.
  • A running wait is not pushed out by more losses; the extra pressure sets the next wait.
  • A site that never unloads gets its census and arrival when its wait ends.
  • No shunning (a site that refuses arrivals for good): pressure alone holds a farmed site to about one a year.
  • A site is watched while a player is within 128 blocks, and nothing arrives into a watched site.

Animals and dragons

  • Beverton-Holt births for herds: a steady level with no overshoot, whatever the forage tuning.
  • Dragons switch to common prey and stop at a refuge of a tenth of capacity, at least 8 animals.
  • A dragon leaves below 0.35 of its food need and settles only where it would get half.
  • Dragon pairs still breed in their lair (your M1), and a dispersing hatchling takes a vacant lair before a newcomer.
  • A newcomer flies in from the nearest unexplored direction, so you can watch it come.
  • Spring dispersal: 5% of adults from herds above 40% of capacity to neighbours below 25%.
  • An empty range is refounded by 12 animals or 5% of capacity, at a quarter per range-year; the permanent extinct mark goes.
  • Herds refill at biology's speed, one calving a year; a larger frontier group could cut a world wipe's recovery by about 40% later.

Villages and Ribbits

  • The sim's village population follows the census both ways, whatever EQ-1 decides. Today the economy trades with dead villagers.
  • Settlers are a family from beyond the explored edge; no village gives any, so nobody you traded with, cured or named is moved.
  • Growth inside a site (breeding, unloaded growth, the Ribbit refill) and pirate hires are not slowed by pressure: beds, houses and crew caps bound them, and they carry no boss loot.
  • Ribbit residents are booked when they exist, and empty houses refill before new ones are built.

Factions and pirates

  • A camp you keep emptying breaks (pressure 2, pool under 10%) and refounds after 48 days doubled by its pressure, capped at 384, with a quarter of its warriors.
  • Pirate crews capped at 18, hired at a quarter a day; ships refit after 4 days doubled by pressure, capped at 96; a haven with no route worth hunting rests.
  • Merchant and navy ships unchanged: their 2-day refill already works.
  • The gate's numbers: a warrior is worth 3 items, revenge at most 10, belief fades over 96 days, scouts every 48 days.

Boundaries

  • Natural Death Worms and natural dragons stay outside the sim (your R18a and OG-1).
  • Notables unchanged; dead ones already cost nothing.
  • Industry districts are made only by players.
  • A ledger row that does not parse is dropped, and left-behind counts are clamped to what the census finds: no format promises before a release.
