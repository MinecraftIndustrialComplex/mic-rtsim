Mostly no: GregTech's lag is in the base you stand in, and rtsim leaves loaded chunks alone

Could rtsim fix the late-game GregTech lag that brings computers to a crawl? A research pass over GTNH (1.7.10) and GregTech CEu Modern (GTM), from issues, pull requests and read-only clones of both. Nothing was built or run. From the research note on main (cee7690).

The famous lag comes from the loaded base: machines re-searching recipes they cannot start, blocks ticking with nothing to do, and power networks that throw away their route caches on every block update. rtsim only acts on unloaded chunks, so it changes none of that. Both GregTech teams are already fixing these causes in their own code, by putting machines to sleep exactly and waking them on the event that matters. GTNH has largely done it; GTM is doing it in 8.0 and 9.0. rtsim fits only the minority case: bases kept chunk-loaded just so they keep producing, such as offline players' bases on a server and remote rigs. Nobody has measured how much lag those cause.

rtsim fits bases kept loaded only to keep producing, a share nobody has measured

That means multiplayer servers that keep offline players' bases chunk-loaded, remote rigs such as oil drills and mining outposts, and a base left loaded while its owner is in another dimension. Unloading those removes their whole cost, and a GregTech plugin could credit their output exactly in rate. GregTech suits rtsim's glass box better than Create does: every recipe has a fixed duration and power draw, and overclocking and parallels are integer math fixed at recipe start.

The catches a plugin would have to handle:

  • AE2 drops a grid node when its chunk unloads, so a grid must be one district, all loaded or all simulated. Autocrafting on request is not simulated, since nobody is requesting while it is unloaded.
  • In GTNH, running out of power mid-recipe voids the recipe's outputs, so the sim must book that loss or it beats reality.
  • GTNH's chance outputs are true random; rtsim would match their mean and drop the variance. GTM's are deterministic and match exactly.
  • GTNH players check output against their calculators, so only the glass box would do: the black box's learned rates replay at or below what was measured, and that shortfall is what they notice first.

Sleeping loaded factories is the wrong tool, because GregTech's cost is in transitions, not steady running

  • A running machine is already cheap: progress plus one, and a power drain. The cost is in recipe searches, output merging and ME flushes, and batch mode already folds those, exactly.
  • A machine stuck searching is steady at zero. Sleeping it until its inputs or power change is GTM's own planned backoff, which needs no rate model.
  • Frozen state shows: progress bars, GUIs, Jade, active textures, sounds and redstone covers would all stop.
  • Hidden state must be written back on wake (progress, power buffer, chance cache, maintenance), through mixins into code that 8.0 is renaming.
  • Skipping ticks breaks tick-counter logic, which GregTech is full of. Aikar on Spigot's tile tick limiter: "skipping ticks actually FULLY BREAKS Some tile entities".

What carries over from rtsim is the discipline, not the mechanism: a cost formula per system, scheduling the next moment a machine can act instead of polling, and running the mod's own formulas. Upstream these are small patches; through rtsim they would be a whole subsystem.

A GTM plugin is the Create plugin again; GTNH is a rewrite that needs its team's consent

GTM on 1.21.1, aimed at 8.0

GTM's only 1.21.1 releases are 7.0.0 and 7.0.2, from July 2025; since then there is only a rolling 8.0 snapshot, no pack was found on it, and 8.0 renames much of the API a plugin would hook. So the realistic target is GTM 8.0 once it ships for 1.21.1, and rtsim's glass-box replay (G2) has to be on main first.

GTNH on 1.7.10

  • Only the Rust engine and its JNI bridge carry over. The whole Java side (32k lines) is NeoForge 21.1 code: capabilities, attachments, chunk status, events and GameTests. 1.7.10 has none of them, so this is a rewrite, not a port.
  • Many bespoke multiblocks (GT++, TecTech, BartWorks and others now inside GT5U) each need a model or a "not learnable" flag.
  • The GTNH team ships everything through its own pack pipeline and has refused automation shortcuts before: "Tick acceleration will not be added for automation purposes" (GTNH#10230). Nothing happens without their consent, and whether they would accept a native library is unknown.
  • And the lag GTNH players complain about is still in the base they stand in.

The closest prior art is Meanwhile, which replays one block at a time

Meanwhile (v0.1.0) ticks a block entity once when its chunk loads, reads what changed in its data, and repeats that for every missed tick, with completions running through the mod's real logic. It moves no items between machines. That gap, flow between machines, is what rtsim's districts and glass box solve. It is the closest shipped Minecraft equivalent of rtsim's replay and is not yet in rtsim's prior-art list.

No mod or proposal was found that simulates GregTech machines while unloaded, or replaces loaded ones with rates, in either line. The fixes that worked elsewhere are the exact-sleep kind: Create: LazyTick backs off idle Create machines, and Captain of Industry made 1,500 idle machines seven times cheaper by skipping recipe searches when inputs had not changed.

Nothing here needs a decision. If you ever want to go further, the note's order is:

  1. Nothing in rtsim: small sleep-and-wake patches in GregTech itself, such as GTM's failed-match backoff and route caches that survive a block update.
  2. An AE2 plugin, the one piece any AE2 pack would use. It is slice G5 on the any-pack page.
  3. A GregTech glass box, only once GTM 8.0 ships for 1.21.1, and only after profiling how much of a real GTM server's load is chunk-loaded bases of offline owners.
