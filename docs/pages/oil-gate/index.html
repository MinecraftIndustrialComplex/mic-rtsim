What a player meets at an oil field now

  1. ArrivingIn Control's desert dragons arrive as before, and the sim leaves natural dragons alone (OG-1). The gate's own arrivals at oil, the field flights, are built but switched off (question 1).
  2. WatchingA fire dragon low on fuel flies to the nearest oil within 3,000 blocks and rests there for an hour of game time. A player who sees one pass learns where oil is.
  3. LairsChoosing a lair weighs the distance to oil. A field with no fire lair within 1,500 blocks gets one fire roost placed near it, once, when a player first comes near.
  4. BuildingA dragon at a field senses every district within 400 blocks of it, once a day. An oil sample, a fuel stock of 1,000 mB or more, and pollution each make it worse. A working district stays resented, and is forgotten 10 days after it stops.
  5. RaidsA grudge sends a raid on the district. The grudge falls on the district and on players whose home is near it. Watched, the dragon comes in person and fights. Unwatched, the industry engine resolves the raid. A dragon low on fuel drinks the tanks first (question 3).
  6. RoofsA flyer's block damage shrinks with the share of the district's machines under a roof. Glass is no roof. The fight and the theft still happen: the dragon lands in the yard.
  7. DryA dragon with an empty reserve cannot breathe fire. It fights with bite and claws, at half strength while the sim resolves the fight.

In Control's desert blazes are untouched (OG-12). Oil in the ground never runs down for a dragon (OG-6): it is in nobody's books. A world with no oil known yet plays as before; the gate opens on the first oil chunk reported.

You answered all 5 on 21 October

Logged as OGB-1 to OGB-5. You reframed question 1, skipped question 4, and took the recommendation on the rest.

1. Field flights: what makes the gate dangerous enough to switch?

Your answer: neither option. The gate is judged by your bar, not by matching In Control, which "might have been overkill".

A real oil pipeline must be infeasible to build, and hard for a semi-casual player to cheese, for example by running in, dropping a contraption as real blocks and running off laying pipe (OGB-1). A cheese audit, not an integration test, is judging the gate against that bar. Its results follow when it finishes.

2. How big is the fuel reserve?

Your answer, as recommended: (a) a point per fire event, capacity 600, refuelling below 240.

A full reserve lasts about three fights of five streams. A dragon that never fights drinks every 360 points, 45 game days of the slow drain. Built (OGC44).

3. May a drinking dragon take more than the theft cap?

Your answer, as recommended: (a) yes, drinking stays free of the cap.

A dry dragon may drink its whole room from one raid, now up to 600,000 mB with the reserve of 600. The tank is the lure that draws a dry dragon to a base (OGB-3).

4. Does a visitor whose body is lost end its visit?

You skipped this, so it took the recommendation: (a) yes, the visit ends.

If a field flight's body is lost, because the session ended or the server crashed, its visit ends. After a restart, field flights draw again from fresh rolls (OGB-4).

5. Should the oil lookup on new chunks get a cheap first check?

Your answer, as recommended: (a) yes, a cheap biome first look.

A chunk none of whose biomes can hold oil under the pack's rule is answered 0 after 504 biome lookups instead of 12,800. Every other chunk keeps the exact getBaseOilAmount call, so the look never changes an answer, and the dieselgenerators GameTest checks it against getBaseOilAmount. Built (OGB-5, OGC45).

Decided while building: 43 choices, each can be undone

Logged as OGC1 to OGC43 in docs/choices-oilgate.md. Every number is a placeholder until measurement sets it.

Roofs

  • The roof gate measures the unroofed share of the district's surveyed machines; ground attacks and unsurveyed districts pass in full (OGC1).
  • A roof stops a flyer's block damage, not its theft or its fight (OGC2).

The reserve

  • Fire dragons only (OGC4). Capacity 100 (600 since OGC44), a point every 3,000 ticks and a point per breath, as a closed form with no per-tick work (OGC5). Every write restarts the time drain (OGC6).
  • The gate opens on the first oil known, with every fire dragon full (OGC12). A roosting dragon wakes to refuel only from above the refuel level (OGC13).
  • A breath is one Ice and Fire fire event, which fires every tick of a stream (OGC29). A dry dragon strikes at half strength (OGC18) with bite and claws: its air attack becomes a tackle and its ground fire a bite (OGC30).

Refuelling

  • Oil chunks are indexed in 32 by 32 chunk regions and joined into fields; amounts are not kept (OGC7).
  • Drinking takes fuel goods first, up to the reserve's room, free of the theft cap (OGC8). Stolen fuel is booked as theft and burnt, so the books balance (OGC9).
  • Refuelling weighs like hunger: the reserve's room minus the flight's cost, within 3,000 blocks; a hungry dragon refuels only when that beats its best prey (OGC14).
  • A low dragon at a field raids the nearby district with the most fuel. If a player watches it, the dragon comes in person, hostile, on the day it first senses the district (OGC15).

Field flights

  • Off by default; the plugin's config sends the switches (OGC10, OGC28).
  • They reproduce In Control's own process: a roll every 10 seconds on average per dimension, for a player within 100 blocks of oil and at or above y 15, at most 200 drawn dragons a dimension and 8 a field, with Ice and Fire's own age, colour and sex rolls (OGC24). A player counts for 200 ticks after his last report (OGC25).
  • Visitors arrive hostile and do not stay: one carrying nothing leaves the sim; one carrying valuables claims a vacant lair or stays a wanderer (OGC11, OGC33).
  • A dragon flying to refuel is seen like a hunting one (OGC19).

Lairs

  • One fire roost placed per field with no fire lair within 1,500 blocks, checked once and saved (OGC20). Placement goes through vanilla's place-structure path, nearest the field first, on a free site with no player within 48 blocks (OGC32).
  • Each new fire lair surveys the oil within 1,024 blocks (OGC21). An oil request in a dimension Java never named is dropped (OGC22). Oil alone now wakes the native dragon engine (OGC27).

Sensing

  • Encroachment is a new report kind: as bad as an assault and lasting 10 days; only dragons witness it, so villages never gossip it (OGC3).
  • Each district is sensed once a day within 400 blocks of a field (OGC16). A grudge raids through the same threat interface as other raids (OGC17).

Cost, core and saves

  • The oil sampler shares 4 samples and 2 ms a tick, and sends only chunks with oil (OGC31).
  • A survey samples only chunks in memory, and the rest when they load. A chunk not in memory cost 79 ms to sample, so one survey of up to 13,000 chunks would have added about 80 ms to every tick for over 15 minutes (OGC43).
  • A change in core, outside the gate: a district that gains a chunk while loaded now tells the engine, so a raid on a new factory breaks what it should; before, it broke nothing until the chunks reloaded (OGC41).
  • New save format with no migration, as there is no backward compatibility before release (OGC23). A dragons_oil scale sweep in the bench (OGC26).
  • Test harness choices: guarded mock players, clocks caught up on GameTest servers, the danger test's three sites, and every counted suite on fresh worlds (OGC34 to OGC40, OGC42).

Next: the underground threat, not built

OG-9e: a Death Worm oil nest, placed on the first load of each oil chunk and asleep until a pumpjack drills, under a new threat class for burrowing attackers. Nothing of it exists yet, but its hooks are in place:

  • Every new oil chunk passes one spot in the engine, whatever reported it: that is where a nest will be queued.
  • A command id is reserved for placing the nest on a chunk's first load.
  • Field flights already stop below y 15, the depth where the nest takes over.
  • A record kind for a drilled chunk's sample is defined, so drilling can reach the engine.
