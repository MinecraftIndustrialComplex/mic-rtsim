Three limits to know up front

  • Far terrain needs DH 3.3.3. DH 3.3.1 switched it off on any client with Chunky, and the pack ships Chunky on clients. The pack now ships DH 3.3.3 (DH-3), and Chunky on servers only. More below.
  • Mac players see none of it. DH skips all box drawing on macOS.
  • Players who turn off DH's "generic rendering" (the switch for its beacons and clouds) see none of it either. The mod notices and asks the server for nothing.

Built on 22 October: trains, the feed and dragons

Phase 1 drew trains from Create's own map sync, client only. Phase 2 replaced that with rtsim's own feed from the server and added dragons. Both are on mic-rtsim main. The new client jar, mic_rtsim_dh, goes into the pack with mic-rtsim's own pack commit, as you approved.

  • rtsim's own feedTwo optional network messages: mic_rtsim:far_sub, the client's subscription (its DH radius, detail setting and object budget), and mic_rtsim:far, the server's answer. Once a second the server sends each player only what changed: a new object, a new flight leg, a move of an eighth of a block, a new entity. A still train or a roosting dragon costs nothing after its first second. A client without the jar joins as before.
  • Trains from the serverThe server reads Create's trains once a second for every player, as Create's own map sync reads them, and sends each player only the nearest within his DH radius. While the feed answers, the client asks Create for nothing. On a server without the feed, phase 1's Create sync still works.
  • DragonsThe engine sends one record per flight leg (FAR_LEG) when a leg is booked or changes, never a pass over every dragon. Near another player the real Ice and Fire dragon replaces the leg on the same handle, and gives it back when it goes, so nothing pops. The client runs one second behind and keeps legs on the server's clock; a hunting dragon circles.
  • Wings as a flipbookA dragon is a body and one wing group per pose, cut for 16 headings. A wingbeat only switches which wing group shows; nothing is cut again. Colours by species, sizes by growth stage, all set by eye for now.
  • The band gap closedBetween the server's simulation distance and your render distance, Create has no carriage entities and vanilla draws over DH, so in phase 1 a train crossing that band vanished. A small vanilla-side renderer now draws the same boxes there, lit and fogged as DH lights its own.
  • Budgets by settingLOW, MEDIUM and HIGH ask for 48, 128 or 320 objects within the DH radius: dragons first (8, 24 or 64), then the nearest carriages. The server caps the radius at 16,384 blocks.

The flipbook, seen from the front (an illustration, not a capture). Each pose is its own group of boxes, cut once; the beat plays up, level, down, level, 4 frames of 5 ticks each, and each dragon starts at its own frame so a flight does not flap in step. LOW has only up and down. A roosting dragon folds its wings and shows the body alone. Boxes cannot rotate, so a turning dragon is cut again for its new heading, one of 16.

With the feed on, Create's sync sent its first 2 packets and nothing after. The connection's compression is not counted.

The frame

  • At the heaviest load, 64 dragons and 96 carriages drawn on HIGH: 2,284 microseconds of drawing, 377 of the controller and 48 of the band pass, 2.7 ms of a 12.5 ms frame (80 fps).
  • One of our groups costs 1.1 to 1.3 times one of DH's own cloud groups in the same window. In phase 1 they cost the same: about 8 microseconds for each carriage drawn. A dragon is two groups, so it costs about two carriages.
  • A wingbeat cuts nothing: 1,235 to 1,261 pose switches in a 10-second window with 24 dragons, against 77 to 90 cuts, from hunters turning.
  • The band pass costs 10 to 48 microseconds with nothing in the band, and about 16 a mover when it draws: 163 for the 10 in view in the band run. If the band ever holds many, caching its cuts as the DH side does is the fix.

The server's sweep

Once a second the server reads the trains and dragons and builds every player's message, all in one tick: 1.1 to 1.4 ms for 24 dragons and 50 trains once the JIT had compiled it. The sends are per player, so by arithmetic, not measured, 10 players would put about 6.5 ms into that one tick. Spreading the players over the second's 20 ticks would flatten it; that is not built, and is the one follow-up to do before a server with many DH players.

Your laptop was in low-power mode

After the first three runs of the round, the GPU held its lowest clock, 600 MHz at 15 W. After the round the laptop's platform profile read low-power, on AC power. Nothing in this work set it, so if you did not, it is probably still on. Other sessions' jobs also held the load average between 10 and 24. So frame rates and GPU time do not compare across runs; what holds is our group against DH's cloud group in the same window.

What was not measured

  • The boxes' GPU time: the whole-frame timer cannot separate it on a shared GPU whose clock changed mid-round.
  • A weak PC, so the budgets for each setting are still set by eye.
  • A dedicated server with remote clients, or more than one player watching: every run was single player.
  • Real Ice and Fire dragons in a client. The bench's dragons are made up; the hand-over between an engine leg and a real body is tested by GameTests.
  • The hide test with real carriages or dragons near the camera.
  • A server running for hours: the longest run was 333 sweeps, about 5.5 minutes.
  • The band's light at night, under the darkness effect, or by block light.

Two traps the bench found: an autosave with the made-up trains crashed the server (Create could not save a carriage with no contraption), so the bench turns saving off while they run (e5fa3fb); and vanilla refuses a simulation distance below 5.

First, far terrain: fixed, with DH 3.3.3 and Chunky on servers only

Distant Horizons 3.3.0 to 3.3.2 switch off far-terrain generation on any client that has Chunky installed, while the config screen still shows it as on. The pack shipped DH 3.3.1 and Chunky 1.4.23, both on clients and servers, so every player's client was affected: far terrain showed only where they had walked.

Before: DH 3.3.1 with Chunky on the client

The client asked for no far terrain, in single player or from a server. Trains and dragons would have floated over a void, which is worse than no far view.

The fix: DH 3.3.3, then Chunky on servers only

The only newer DH for 1.21.1. It switches far terrain off only while Chunky is actually generating. It resets DH's config file, which the pack does not ship. Whether it breaks another mod in the pack is untested.

You chose DH 3.3.3 (DH-3), and the pack moved to it (62dd14e). DH 3.3.3 then turned out to drop a client that has Chunky from a dedicated server about 10 seconds after joining, so the pack also made Chunky server-only (d31abf3): single-player clients lose /chunky, dedicated servers keep it. Nothing the far view needs is newer than DH 3.3.1, so the renderer works on either version, and no DH version, released or in development, adds rotation.

Nobody has done this before

No mod draws Create trains, windmills or dragons through Distant Horizons. The only confirmed user of DH's box API is LOD Entity Rendering, for 1.20.1, which draws every entity as one box and sends every position to every player every tick; the far view filters and throttles instead. Separate Sable Render Distance stretches Sable's own range, which sends full ship data to far players; a silhouette is far cheaper. Nothing to reuse beyond DH's API itself.

Decided in the research; each can be undone

  • No test mod was built for the research: the jars answered how to draw, whether rotation exists and what costs a frame. Phases 1 and 2 then measured it.
  • The feed and the client list live in rtsim's core with no pack mod in them; each source sits in its own mod's plugin; the renderer is its own client-only plugin.
  • The first slice reused Create's map sync; phase 2 replaced it with our own filtered feed. Phase 2's own choices are DHT25 to DHT39.
  • Rotation by flipbooks and re-cuts, not by replacing DH's shader, which would also take over its beacons and clouds and fight shader packs for the same slot.
  • Near the player, a proxy hides as soon as the real thing exists on the client, or its chunk is loaded.
  • NPC ships are not drawn until they have bodies. Smoke is solid first, see-through only once it is seen to work.
  • Bands mustering at camp or walking home are not drawn, since a player who rode up to one would find nothing there today.
