# TALK choices

Reversible choices made while designing and building TALK (TALK.md), each with its reason. Ben's
questions TQ-1 to TQ-3 (TALK.md §13) were answered on 2026-10-22, in his words:

- TQ-1: "Talk wins on talkers (Recommended)"
- TQ-2: "Nothing"
- TQ-3: "Generated names and directions, both are cool ideas!"

| Id | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| TK1 | A villager's first talk of the day with a player adds **MINOR_POSITIVE 3**; "a day" is 24,000 ticks of game time | vanilla drops values below 2 on decay, so +2 would vanish at the first decay; +3 nets +2 a day against vanilla's decay of 1, up to vanilla's max of 25 (reputation +25, a fifth of a cure). Game time, not day time, so `/time set` and sleeping do not reset the cap |
| TK2 | A ribbit's first talk of the day with a player adds **+3** to its village's sentiment toward them (journaled TALKED, the phase 1 input `Talked`); the cap is per ribbit, not per village | the sim's own feeling is the ribbits' relationship; weak feelings fade in hours, so talk lifts a player over the sorcerers' -25 for the day and never makes a friend alone. Per ribbit reuses the villagers' attachment; a village of ten ribbits gives at most +30 in a day, which fades by the next |
| TK3 | Rate limits: a talker answers one player at most once per 40 ticks, and anyone once per 10; a click inside the window is cancelled silently. In memory | no spam, no saved state |
| TK4 | The picker's weights (TALK.md §5.6): fluff 1 (+0.5 a condition met), mood 6 for a raid now, 4 afraid, 3 monsters near at night, 3 hurt, 2 uneasy, 1.5 prices, hunger, liked or disliked (3 hated); news 3 x freshness x 2 about the player x 1.5 about us. A topic's weight is per level of the voice's chain (TK31), so a farmer's fluff weighs 1 + 0.6 | news and danger should dominate when they exist, fluff fill the quiet; all TUNABLE |
| TK5 | Java resends an open TALK_ASK each tick, at most 32 open, and gives up after 20 ticks, then speaks without facts | a sim switched off (U10) or not running still gets an answer |
| TK6 | Talking to caravan merchants, bandits, raiders, goblins, envoys and Umvuthana changes no feeling | a camp's anger is its memories and grievances (CONFLICT-LIFE); chatting must not talk a war down. Ben asked for the relationship for villagers; ribbits are villagers |
| TK7 | The gesture: crouch, main hand, **empty main hand**, any off hand; `EntityInteract` at HIGHEST on both sides; both hands cancelled on a talker | an item in hand keeps its own use (a name tag, the ribbits' amethyst shard home, a backpack's mob catcher); HIGHEST beats every mod that opens a screen (the audit, TALK.md §3) |
| TK8 | A Create seated shopkeeper never talks | Create cancels the click on the client, so no packet reaches the server; a shopkeeper is Create's |
| TK9 | Not talkers: the Frostmaw, Mowzie's Sculptor, golems, animals, pets, trader llamas, rctmod's trainers, Cobblemon NPCs, and beasts in a band (ravagers, wolves) | beasts do not talk; the Sculptor is one quest NPC with its own screen; Cobblemon is leaving the pack (D4) |
| TK10 | TALK's kind ranges: 0x00C0 to 0x00CF, 0x0A00 to 0x0AFF, 0x8A00 to 0x8AFF | the next free family ranges (DHT26); 0x0B00 and 0x8B00 stay free |
| TK11 | ReportKind ids from 10: 10 to 15 conflict news (Feud, PactSworn, PactBroken, Raid, Fell, a spare), 16 to 23 ports (16 to 20 the ports agent's, 21 to 23 spare), 24 to 31 TALK's own, 32 and up free (ask first); `from_u16` a match and the per-kind tables sized by the highest id | blocks per owner so parallel branches never take the same id; gaps need a match, not `id - 1` |
| TK12 | The bandit's "written warning" (CONFLICT-LIFE.md §2.7, the Warn row) becomes a bandit lookout on the road who speaks the warning when talked to | a written note would be text pushed at the player, or a block or item of our own (D3); a spoken line when they ask is CL-1's rule for envoys |
| TK13 | Names are composed on the server from two-part lists in the core's lang file and sent as plain text | the same name for every player and session, and for the envoy and the villagers; TQ-3 answered both, so names are one of two styles (TK29) |
| TK14 | News whose place is within 96 blocks of the holder is told as seen, the rest as heard | the board keeps no first-hand flag; the place is a fair proxy (the witness radius is 32, a village's own news falls inside 96) |
| TK15 | Lines are lang keys `mic_rtsim.talk.<voice>.<topic>.<n>`, counted once at server start, drawn from the voice's chain of levels (TK31); sent as `translatableWithFallback` with the server's English | adding a line is adding a key; a client without the lang file still reads English |
| TK16 | Babies talk (a `child` voice, fluff only); sleeping and busy (trading with another player) talkers say only a state line and give no credit; a hurt talker may say so (weight 3) and still credits | a crouching click always gets an answer, and a credit needs a real conversation, which a hurt villager can still have |
| TK17 | The talker looks at the player for 40 ticks (a villager's `LOOK_TARGET` memory; any other mob's look control, set again each tick for the 40, since look goals overwrite a one-off look) and plays its ambient sound | the conversation shows in the world; the sound carries no text, so others nearby learn nothing |
| TK18 | A talk with a villager or wandering trader counts in vanilla's "Talked to Villagers" statistic | vanilla's own name for it; a trade attempt counts there today |
| TK19 | Mood thresholds: reputation liked at 25, disliked at -25, hated at -100 (golems); uneasy for an attack on us within 10 days, afraid within 2; a good dear under half its target, cheap over twice; hungry when the scarce good is food under a third | vanilla's own reputation steps, rtsim's penalty levels, and the report lifetimes; all TUNABLE |
| TK20 | At most four news items an answer, ranked kind weight x severity x freshness x (3 about the player, 2 about us) | four is enough for the picker to choose among; the board is at most 256, so the scan is cheap |
| TK21 | A villager outside an adopted village has no holder: fluff and its own mood only | no board to speak from; a stray trader (not a puppet) likewise |
| TK22 | Each player's last 8 line keys are skipped while another variant is left; in memory | "never the same line back to back", across talkers of one voice too |
| TK23 | The envoy says the same line each time until its demand is settled, then a settled line | it is one line (CL-1), the demand's own words |
| TK24 | M3's `ConflictNews.post` hook is not needed by TALK | news is reports on the engine's boards; Java reads them through TALK_FACTS when asked |
| TK25 | The ribbit credit and the villager cap share one saved attachment, `mic_rtsim:talk_days` (player to the last day credited, at most 16 players) | the `mic_rtsim:gossip_ops` precedent; NeoForge drops it when rtsim is removed (UR-1) |
| TK26 | Slice 2 records Feud (10) where FACTION_NOTICE fires today, Raid (13) at a raid's and a skirmish's resolution and Fell (14) at a chief's or champion's death; PactSworn and PactBroken (11, 12) stay unrecorded until alliances are built | no CONFLICT-LIFE builder is running (main, 2026-10-22); alliances exist only in the model |
| TK27 | No hint that talk exists, and no line in the pack's guide: players find it themselves. Slice 1 has no hint code and no `mic_rtsim:talk_hinted` attachment | Ben's answer to TQ-2, "Nothing" (2026-10-22); it replaces the one-time hint built on (a) |
| TK28 | `RtsimApi.registerTalker` takes the entity type's id (a `ResourceLocation`), callable from mod construction; several talkers on one type are asked in registration order, the first whose `talks` says yes speaks | plugins of soft dependencies (Mowzie) name types by id without touching the mod's classes, and a vanilla type (a pillager) can be a conflict body's talker and nobody else's |
| TK29 | Names or descriptions, per ref and per line: the listener is "you", other players are named; the talker's own settlement or camp, anything it saw and anything under 600 blocks (a short walk) are named; under 3,000 blocks (a day's ride) a ref is named half the time, farther a quarter of the time, and otherwise described by its people and a locator (the biome at its place and the compass, or a distance band). A described ref at the report's own place drops its locator. Distance bands: right here under 24 blocks, close by under 128, a short walk under 600, a day's ride under 3,000, far off beyond | Ben's answer to TQ-3 ("both are cool ideas"): near and seen places are the ones people know by name, far ones are rumour; randomness keeps both styles in play. Thresholds TUNABLE |
| TK30 | A TALK_FACTS ref carries where it is (dx, dz from the talker, flag POS), so a ref is 32 bytes and a news item 120 | a description's locator ("the goblins in the hills") needs the camp's place, which is not the report's place |
| TK31 | A topic draws from every level of the voice's chain that has lines: the most specific at the topic's weight, each parent at 0.6 of the level below; `any` only when nothing more specific has lines. Plugin talkers are asked before the core's own; a talker that does not chat (hostile, envoy) gets no core fluff or mood | a farmer says the farmer's lines and the villagers' shared ones too (the review found the shared ones unreachable); a plugin can take over a vanilla type; slice 4's hostile bodies and the envoy speak only their own lines |
| TK32 | With phase 1 off (U10) an ask is answered at once, without HOLDER, instead of not at all | TALK.md 9.3 says "nothing answers" and Java waits 20 ticks (TK5); an answer with no flags makes Java speak fluff now, so a switched-off engine costs the player nothing. The engine is running in that case, so it can say "I know nothing" itself |
| TK33 | News ranking, in one place (`kind_weight`, `severity_term`, `freshness`, `rank`): score = kind weight x severity term x freshness x about, all integers. Kind weight in tenths: Trade 3, Killing, ResidentKilling, Raid, Fell, Feud, PortRaided and HavenFell 15, the rest 10. Severity term: a severity of 25 or more counts as it is (their base-delta scale, 100 and up); a severity under 25, of any kind, is a rung and counts 100 + 25 x the rung, rung capped at 4; the news kinds (10 and up) are always rungs. Freshness: 1000 falling linearly to 250 over the kind's lifetime, 250 after it. About: 3 when `who` or `whom` is the asker, 2 when it is the holder, else 1 (not stacked, the larger wins). Ties: newer first, then the packed row bytes ascending, so the order never depends on the board's order. The top four are sent, best first | spec 9.3 gives the shape and says the weights are TUNABLE; the news kinds carry a rung or an outcome in severity rather than a multiple of 100, so they need their own term or a rung 1 feud would weigh 1; the conflict also records some old kinds by rung (Robbery 2, Rescue 2, Killing 4), which on the base scale would rank below a fair trade of 100, so anything under 25 is read as a rung whatever its kind (the review of slice 2a). A fixed tie-break keeps two engines and a replay in step |
| TK34 | News from another frame than the holder's report place: dx = dz = 0, no SEEN, and `near` is worked out without POS. Refs in another frame carry no POS either | there is no short way round between two levels; zeros say "no direction" and POS is the flag that says whether dx and dz mean anything |
| TK35 | `near` is the nearest Village (not a base or a haven) within 768 blocks (three market cells) of the report's place, found by the phase 1 market cells, seams crossed; ties go to the lower key; none within that is class 0 | spec 9.3 says "the nearest settlement by the phase 1 market cells"; a base or a haven is not a place a villager names; 768 is as far as the cells reach in three steps and is TUNABLE |
| TK36 | Ref resolution by the actor's kind and key: Settlement of a faction key is class 5 (camp; a camp's Haven node shares the faction key); a Haven that is not a faction is class 9; a Village is class 2 with culture "human" or "ribbit" (dictionary 5); a PlayerBase is class 3 (b the owner); a Group is, in order: the settlers' key or an oil district (class 0), a faction (camp), a lair (key xor `LAIR_TAG`, class 8, what the species body), otherwise a caravan (class 4, POS while it lives, DEAD when it is gone) provided a caravan with that seed lives or the conflict runs: a camp's group actor survives the camp, and with the conflict off nothing can say it was a camp, so an unrecognised group is class 0 then, not a dead caravan. A group keyed with the lair or oil district prefix is never a caravan (a lair the ecology does not hold, class 0). Where a conflict that runs has lost a camp, the camp is still told as a dead caravan: nothing in the tables tells the two apart. A Notable is a chief (class 6, a its seed, b the faction key) when its home is a camp, a caravan trader (class 4) when its home is a village, the haven itself (class 9, a the haven key, POS) when its home is a haven or a base, so a port's pirate captain is "the pirates of that haven" and not a trader whose caravan is gone, and a dragon (class 7, a its seed, b its lair key, what the species body) when its home is a lair. A player is class 1 with the UUID, or a = 0 and b = the UUID hash when only the hash is known. A dragon's locator is its lair's position, a chief's is its camp's. For a caravan holder, "us" in TO_US and BY_US is both the caravan's own actor and its home village's, so a report about its village is about it. | one lookup each, no module asked to describe itself (that would reach into four crates); each rule is what the owning module registers the actor with today; the caravan's home counts as us because a caravan tells what happened to its village as its own news (the review of slice 2a) |
| TK37 | A handle that no longer validates (a fallen chief, a slain dragon; `Knowledge::die` removes the actor) is class 0 with flag DEAD. A dragon with no lair is class 0. Ship refs (class 10) are never produced yet | the actor is gone, so its kind and key cannot be read back; the lair is the only place a lairless dragon would be described from; no ship actors are registered in knowledge today |
| TK38 | Holder kinds 3 (faction) and 4 (ship), and any kind this build does not know, are answered without HOLDER until slice 4 (the camp and the ship are not holders of a board yet) | Java speaks fluff and its own mood for them, as it does for an unknown holder; the codec accepts the kinds so slice 4 changes the engine only |
| TK39 | Ask bookkeeping: at most 256 asks wait (a later one is dropped and counted, Java sends it again), the last 1,024 answered askIds are remembered (oldest forgotten first), and all of it is cleared at SESSION_START. An askId of 0, a dimension above 65,535 and a record that does not decode are refused and counted, logged when the count reaches a power of two | Java sends an open ask every tick, so the same id comes back until the answer lands; a bounded set keeps a stuck client from growing the engine; askIds are per session (spec 9.2) |
| TK40 | Test order opcode 40, `TalkReport`, posts one report for a village from a test: village, kind, severity, flags, who and whom as (class, a, b), place x and z, and ago in ticks. It first checks everything that needs no actor: phase 1 on, the kind known, the village adopted, x and z within the pack's range (-8,388,608 to 8,388,607, `rtsim_knowledge::report::COORD_LIMIT`), the report not already past its lifetime, and room in the table (the report would outrank the least important one); then it resolves the actors as TK36 does (a camp, lair, village, caravan or player; a caravan nobody has seen registers a Group actor), taints phase 1 (a player or a caravan actor may have been registered, whether the order goes on or not), records the report at now minus ago, makes the village witness it, and drops the vanilla gossip that this report wrote and no other module's. It refuses (counted) on any of those checks and when an actor does not resolve | the fake Java in rtsim-host keeps no 0x8A00 payloads, and slice 2 does not record news yet (Feud, Raid, Fell are slice 2's second commit), so a protocol test needs a way to put news on a board; 40 is clear of every order any branch takes (30 to 39 are left to the other branches); a refusal that has read no actor must leave the world as it was, so it cannot make the next save a snapshot, and dropping only this report's gossip keeps what the conflict or the ports queued in the same step (the review of slice 2a) |
| TK41 | `LAIR_TAG` is copied into talk.rs, pinned by a test that registers a real lair and checks the actor's key; the oil district prefix (`DIST_TAG`) is copied too and cannot be pinned (it is private in rtsim-ecology) | exporting either would change rtsim-ecology, which this slice may not touch; a test that fails when the tag moves is the cheaper guard. A later change can export both and delete the copies |
| TK42 | Port state is about the holder: the latest PortClosed or PortReopened whose `whom` is the holder decides PORT_CLOSED (a tie is open); BLOCKADED is a Blockade about the holder strictly newer than both | spec 9.3 says "about the holder"; the ports agent writes `whom` as the port, so `whom` is the holder. Strict comparisons mean two events in one tick favour the quieter reading |
| TK43 | The answer is worked out at the write point (after simulate), not when the ask is read | the ask is read before the step simulates, and a report that arrives in the same step should be in it; the order changes no state, so nothing depends on when it is read |
| TK44 | An answer that could never fit a command buffer (more than the buffer's whole capacity, or a dictionary name whose record is over 65,528 bytes, the test a resent transfer's names get) is dropped and logged, not retried | it cannot happen with four news items, and a stuck head of the queue would hold every later ask back; Java's 20-tick wait (TK5) covers the player |
| TK45 | The holder's sentiment is read from an existing player actor (found by UUID hash), never created for the ask; a player the engine has never met reads 0 | asking must not grow the knowledge tables, and a stranger has no feeling toward them |
| TK46 | Scarcest and most glutted goods are the lowest and highest stock per target in thousandths over the village's market, leaving out currencies and goods with no target; ties go to the lower good id; when the scarcest and the most glutted are the same good (one good has a target, or the stocks are all equal) only the scarcest is named and the glutted is 0; a node that is not a storehouse (a player base) gives none | the spec says only "scarcest" and "most glutted"; per target is the scale the market's own curve uses, and a good nobody targets is never short or glutted; the same good cannot be both what the village lacks most and has most of, and a talker that says both would contradict itself (the review of slice 2a) |
| TK47 | A news item's and a ref's dx and dz are in the talker's own chart: the image of the place nearest the talker (`Topology::toward`) less the talker, not `Topology::displacement` (which is a vector in the canonical chart) | on a glued world a talker standing in a turned or slid tile reads its map in that tile's axes, so a canonical vector would point it the wrong way; on a flat or wrapped world the two agree |
| TK48 | A news kind's weight in a notable's memory (salience) and in the report table's importance is `rung_weight(severity)` = 100 + 25 x the rung, rung capped at 4, the same formula the ranking uses (`rtsim_knowledge::report::rung_weight`); old kinds keep their severity as it is | otherwise a rung 4 raid was a salience of 4 and the first thing a full table evicted, below every old kind at 100; the table's persisted importance order changes meaning (nothing is migrated: the pack is unreleased) |
