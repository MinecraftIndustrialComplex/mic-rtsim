mic-rtsim / design review / 2026-10-20

Oil behind guns, coin that builds, ships that sail on, and a map to watch it all: four designs to approve

Four mic-rtsim designs, checked against the pack's jars and the code, none built yet. The first three change play; the fourth is an ops-only viewer of world state, shared with Deep Time. Each section shows what a player or an op does, then how it works. They lean on each other and on one engine, so they are reviewed together. You are asked for 55 decisions: the answer sheet at the end has every recommendation preselected, so change only what you disagree with and copy your answers back.

Urgent, and already fixed on main

While the Ice and Fire plugin ran, the sim was deleting the desert's dragons

Rule R18 discarded natural and spawner dragons. Ice and Fire has no natural dragon spawns of its own, so in this pack every natural fire dragon is one of In Control's desert dragons. With mic_rtsim_iceandfire installed, the desert gate was gone, and oil was no longer behind guns.

P13 already says In Control stays untouched until the sim is measured to be at least as dangerous. So OG-1's recommendation, leave those dragons alone until the gate is measured, was applied as a bug fix and is merged on main (f55af8c): an engine setting, adopt_natural_dragons, default false, leaves those dragons as plain Ice and Fire mobs (R18a). It is to be set true only after OG-10's measurement. Pick OG-1 (c) on the sheet if you want the discard back.

§1 / P13 / OILGATE.md / OG-1 to OG-13

The oil gate: dragons drink the oil, sense industry near it, and the deep guards it

"This needs a careful touch, the idea behind this system was. A) Restrict oil to desert biomes. B) Make desert biomes extremely hostile to players s.t. they would need guns or a similar level of progression in the tech tree in order to soft-lock oil behind guns."

"Danger follow deposits, though potentially make it a bit more interesting."

Ben, P13

Deep Time will put oil anywhere, so the gate follows deposits, not biomes. Three rules carry it: dragons need oil to breathe fire, so they drink and nest near it; they sense players starting industry near it; and the layer under every field listens for anyone who digs. A player with iron gear and a bow can visit an oil field and run. Holding one takes guns.

In game: Mara goes for oil

  1. Scan. The Diesel oil scanner reads "high" in the dunes east of Brindle.
  2. Flights. Within the first minute a stage-2 fire dragon flies in from the edge of view, then another. She runs.
  3. A drinker. Cinderjaw, whose roost is 1,100 blocks away, rests on the sand over the field: its reserve ran low.
  4. A base. A week later, with a Model 943, she builds near the field. A district forms, and Cinderjaw gets an Encroachment report.
  5. The deep. Her well shaft breaks into sculk at y -40. Four warnings, and a warden rises. She comes back at night with wool, sneaks, and breaks the shriekers.
  6. The raid. Crude fills a tank and Cinderjaw's grudge sets. She logs off; unwatched, Cinderjaw takes 4,000 mB from the tank and two exposed blocks.
  7. The answer. She roofs the well and adds a turret. Raids still take oil, but break nothing under the roof.

OILGATE §5 A. Under Deep Time (§5 B), a trap under a forest 3,000 blocks from any desert gets the same gate: indexed at world load, a roost placed near it, flights and sculk.

§2 / E-sink / SINK.md / MS-1 to MS-11

The money sink: coin over the ceiling buys lamps, golems and tribute

"Surplus coin above a treasury's target is not destroyed. It buys something real and visible instead: village upgrades, buildings, tribute."

E-sink; Ben chose "Sink into something real"

Round 2's sink made closed worlds go quiet by deleting coin above twice a treasury's target. E-sink keeps that law exactly and changes only where the coin goes: into a works purse that nothing in the market reads, which buys pieces the village's own structures already use. Every market sees the same trajectory, and conservation stays exact.

In game: Brindle lights its streets, Oakford pays the dragon

  1. Rich. You buy Brindle's bread with emeralds you mined; its neighbours pay for its wheat. Its treasury reaches twice its target.
  2. Unchanged. The excess goes into Brindle's purse. Its trades, prices and caravans behave exactly as before.
  3. Built. The purse reaches a plains lamp's price. As Brindle's chunks load, the lamp post rises near the square, a few layers a tick, never on your fences.
  4. Raided. Cinderjaw takes Oakford's cows in winter. Next season Oakford pays a quarter of its purse into Cinderjaw's hoard, and its cows are spared.
  5. Shifted. Cinderjaw hunts the road between Oakford and Brindle instead. Caravans lose cargo; Brindle's prices for Oakford's goods rise.
  6. Robbed. You fly to the roost: Oakford's emeralds sit by Ice and Fire's treasure. Kill Cinderjaw and the tribute lapses; Oakford buys a golem.

SINK §6 A and B. A ribbit village (§6 C) does the same in amethyst, buying Ribbits' own lamp posts; its houses still come only from prosperity (P17).

  1. 1 / PurseCrossed the ceiling, not yet spentOut of circulation: a number nobody reads.
  2. 2 / WorksA lamp, decor or a golem is boughtLeaves as the work's blocks. Breaking a lamp post yields its blocks, never emeralds.
  3. 3 / TributePaid each season to a raiding dragonCoin in a chest inside the lair, placed when the lair loads.
  4. 4 / A playerRobs the hoardThe coin re-enters the economy only when the player trades it.

Conservation, per currency: the treasuries, coin in transit, caravan cargo and the purses together always equal genesis plus minted plus what players brought in, less what players took out, what was destroyed, what works cost and what tribute paid. Asserted after every event in debug builds, as today.

MS-2, MS-6Need first, then cheapestTribute if a dragon raided within the last season, then a golem if raided and under its cap, then lamps and decor, cheapest first.
MS-8Golems are deterrenceVanilla's own village golem: defends, never despawns, turns on a player at reputation -100. In the sim it raises dragons' village_risk.
M3, M4No growth by the back doorNo beds, workstations, bells or houses: no work changes population or the market. Ribbit houses stay P17's (MS-4).
MS-9A full catalogue keeps savingOnce the caps are reached the purse keeps growing, out of circulation, as the fund M3 and M4 can spend later.

§3 / I17, C30, C32, G-B7 / VOYAGE.md / V-1 to V-13

Voyages: a ship that repeats its route keeps sailing while you are away

"I fully expect players to set things up s.t. they have sable ships doing logistics. I.E. A sailbot going between two ports to move vaults filled with items betewen them."

Ben, C30

"On my minecraft server I'm runnin with friends a player decided to create a farm with ~2k sheep on a create airship, he is planning on having it exclusively interact with the rest of the world via another airship farrying it's cargo back and forth. Make sure this kind of bullshittery is supported."

Ben, C32

Today a Sable ship freezes the moment a chunk under it unloads, and nothing aboard moves while it is held. The voyage plugin never reads the autopilot. It learns the route from what the ship does, turns it into a timetable whose position is a closed form of the clock, and runs it while nobody watches. When a player can see where the ship should be, the frozen copy is loaded, teleported there and released (G-B7).

In game: Ana's sailbot, Skylark

  1. Hand-flown. Ana flies Skylark once by hand while building the docks: "not learnable: no repeating route".
  2. Programmed. Her CC computer flies three round trips while she watches. Connectors lock at both docks; funnels fill and empty the four vaults.
  3. Learned. The third trip matches the first two, so the checksum window passes: Skylark is "simulated".
  4. Away. She logs off. Skylark freezes over the desert, mid-leg, and the sim takes it: 7 round trips by arithmetic in the next hour.
  5. Raided. On the 5th trip sky pirates cross its leg. Lanchester against its one cannon: 2 stacks of iron lost, no blocks broken.
  6. Back. She logs in. Skylark is 300 blocks out, inbound. It is loaded, teleported onto its path, and her program, restarted, flies it home.

Skylark: 7 round trips, 7 vault loads of iron ore delivered, 2 stacks lost to sky pirates

V-2, V-3Calls and portsA call is a docking connector reaching LOCKED, else a dwell within 2 blocks for 200 ticks (PLACEHOLDERS). A port is wherever goods really cross: funnels, belts, arms and deployers reach across the ship boundary.
V-6Running dry is a breakpointEach leg draws its learned fuel. At zero the ship is stranded on its path; on load, Sable decides: hot air hangs, thrusters fall.
V-7Ferries with moving partnersA hovering partner is a fixed dock. A partner on its own voyage is met at one quadratic root per meeting, periodic again when the periods are commensurate.
V-12World wrap needs a Sable adapterToroidal World folds entities, not ships. Plot space, near 20.48 million, must never be folded. Neither mod is in the pack yet.

§4 / VW1 to VW4 / VIEWER.md / VWD-1 to VWD-18

The world viewer: a god view for ops that never touches the game it watches

"I'd like to have a web viewer (probably make a shared one with deeptime) for being able to view the state of a world. (Where various entities are, events between factions, viewing factory logistics from a high level, etc.)"

Ben, VW1; then chose "Ops and devs only"

"Would it be worth making it so we can run the rust sym engine independently from the server for dev purposes?"

Ben, VW2; the design's answer is yes: the headless host

One shared viewer, mic-viewer, grows out of Deep Time's slippy map and HTTP server. For rtsim it runs as a sidecar process at idle priority, reached through an SSH port forward. The game server's thread does no viewer work at all. With nobody watching, the engine's only viewer work is a lease check every 10 s, plus the archive's recording. A headless host runs a copy of a world's sim at any speed with the same viewer attached.

In use: Ben looks at his server

  1. Forward. ssh -L 8740:127.0.0.1:8740 ben@<server>. The viewer listens on the server's loopback only.
  2. Start. rtsim-cli view --live /path/to/world prints what it found and serves at idle priority. Its one write is a lease file.
  3. Open. http://localhost:8740/: Surveyor's terrain where recorded, HABITAT's 64-block cells elsewhere, layers grouped by module.
  4. Zoom out. Villages sized by population, roads by goods in transit and tinted by raid hazard, districts by state. Past 2,000 marks the server sends clusters.
  5. Click a caravan. Zoomed in, caravans move. Its card: home, leg and progress, members, cargo, purse, the news it carries. "Follow" keeps it centred.
  6. Scrub back. The timeline shows events per game day. Filter to Conflict, click last week's Raid spike: the war band's route, the raid's row, the feud's steps before it.
  7. Close the tab. The stream closes, the lease lapses, and the engine's view tap stops within 40 s. Ctrl-C ends the sidecar; nothing is left running.

VIEWER §1.1. On a Deep Time world (§1.2) the base map is the bake's own tiles and wraps east and west. The headless host (§1.3) is rtsim-cli run <copy> --fork <dir> --speed max --until +3650d --view 127.0.0.1:8741.

VWD-11Read only, and testedEvery view read takes &self. A lockstep test runs a world twice, once with a viewer hammering every route: state hashes must agree every game day and the sim pages end byte for byte the same.
VWD-7Locked to the opLoopback only, the Host header checked on every request (no DNS rebinding), a Unix socket where others have shells, and no POST at all.
VWD-6, VWD-13The headless host replays what it knowsA fork of the pages, Java's commands answered as failed. No chunk sampling, puppets or live Create, so only learned or compiled models replay. Ten game years in about 90 s (ESTIMATE).
VWD-3, VWD-17Any world, any pluginSurveyor's 1-block terrain where it recorded, HABITAT's cells underneath. Parts are self-describing rows, so a live sidecar draws a plugin's layers without its code.

What Deep Time would change and expose (§14, after Deep Time's review)

  1. worldmap.js moves to mic-viewer as map.js. window.DTWB becomes a host object passed at creation; the DOM ids become parameters.
  2. projection.js stays a twin, unchanged. The core needs only canonical; the Lambert grid becomes the projection's optional grid.
  3. Tiles need a home. BakeMap imports deeptime-core, so it is no light move: (a) a small deeptime-tiles crate, the default, or (b) tiles over loopback from deeptime watch. That is VWD-18. Its 8 Rust tests move with it.
  4. http.rs gives way to the shared server: a Host check on every GET, and loopback only unless --allow-tailnet.
  5. SSE: stream_events moves as a generic writer; live.rs stays Deep Time's.
  6. Tests stay in Deep Time. worldmap.js's shot test clicks #worldbtn and checks #worldview, so those ids are kept or the test changes with the move.
  7. A dependency: mic-viewer, pinned by git commit.
  8. Optional: an event source over Deep Time's event log for the shared timeline.
  9. P1-19 untouched: nothing new runs during a bake.
  10. Licensing: the moved files leave LGPL-3.0-or-later for MPL-2.0, by your relicensing.

Also from the review: the sidecar's tile cache stays outside the world folder, keyed by the bake's identity, so no tile PNGs land in the server's world or its backups (§9, §13).

Your call, not a proposal

Relicensing Deep Time's moved map code from LGPL to MPL-2.0 is yours to do

VW3 put the shared viewer in a new MPL-2.0 repo, and Deep Time's map core moves there. Deep Time's code is LGPL-3.0-or-later, so the moved files change licence only by your relicensing as their copyright holder, recorded in a NOTICE.md as mic-native's was (A18). No agent can do that for you. Add a note to VWD-2 on the sheet if you want it handled differently.

Risk: unchecked

If TaCZ r6 will not load the gun recipes, nothing sits behind the gate

The pack defines 54 gun recipes. The gun-crafting plan says they failed to load under TaCZ r2; on r6 nobody has checked. If they still fail, the only gun in the game is the spawn bonus chest's Model 943, and only when that world option is on. Whether any gun input needs oil is also unchecked. OILGATE §7 test 5 settles it before the gate's tuning is trusted.

What a gun costs today: a gun smith table by Create mechanical crafting (6 steel plates, 2 steel blocks, 2 anvils, 1 precision mechanism, 1 brass plate, 4 planks). The earliest guns, taurus943, hk_mk23 and springfield1873, need one precision mechanism, and every gun needs a Create Big Cannons autocannon barrel and breech.

Also stale: the pack's Aeronautics and Propulsion notes cite versions 1.1.3 and 1.1.4 (the pack has 1.3.2 and 1.1.5), and INDUSTRY.md still says player ships stay frozen, which I17 and C30 replaced.
