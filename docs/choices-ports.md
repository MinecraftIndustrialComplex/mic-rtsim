# Choices: havens and ports that rise and fall (S8b)

Slice S8b of EQUILIBRIUM.md (§2.4.6a), started 2026-10-22 on branch `ports/lifecycle` after Ben's
answer to T37: "Huh, shouldn't ports be able to get raided, shut down, have new ones form, etc?"
Each row can be undone. Ben answered PORTS-1 and PORTS-2 on 2026-10-22 (EQUILIBRIUM.md §5.1,
DECISIONS.md): navies break havens (his exception, for havens only, to §2.4.5's rule that only
players break a faction), and trade ports close under blockade and reopen when it lifts (not the
recommendation). Ids: PC1 to PC10 the design, PC11 to PC19 the build of S8b-1, PC20 onward the
blockade and closure.

No backward compatibility before a release: the save format stays `RTS2`, haven rows gain fields
without a migration (no saved world holds a haven: no plugin sends HAVEN_FOUND, T36).

| Id | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| PC1 | **An unwatched hunt pays its toll when the pirate reaches the lane**: the square law of its strength against the defence it judged the lane by (one merchant of the medium plus patrols and escorts, T35's D), `crew x (1 - square_law(A, D))` men in thousandths. The pool makes it good; what the pool lacks comes off the crew in whole men, rounded up. A pirate in view (embodied) pays nothing here: its real fights are reported. | The engine's fights are certain (Lanchester), so the cost of a hunt is the law's, not a roll; T35's D is the fight the pirate itself believed it was taking on, so belief and cost agree. One merchant per hunt is the least a hunt that takes any share must meet. |
| PC2 | **Fed for 30 days** (`ShipTunables::starve_days`): a haven is fed while one of its pirates began a hunt within 30 days; starving, its pool turns toward 0 at the hire rate (`pool e^(-r t / cap)`), one stored tick (`fed_until`) marking the turn. | Six rest-and-decide cycles of a pirate (a day's rest, a fruitless decision, a day's rest...) is long enough that a lane briefly escorted does not starve a haven, short enough that a haven with no prey does not sit manned for years. |
| PC3 | **A pirate is laid up at a fruitless decision while its haven starves**: crew ashore, slot empty with no refit booked. | Without it a starving haven's ships rest in port for ever; laying up at a decision needs no new event. |
| PC4 | **A shut haven** has no active ship and is broken or starving; it books no refit and the census counts it `broken`. A fed haven with every ship sunk is not shut (S8's refits still run). | Keeps S8's kill-all numbers: a player's kill-all is answered by refits, not by refounding. |
| PC5 | **Refound after `min(384, 48 x 2^P)` days** (`ShipTunables::refound`, half-life 96), the camps' rule (§2.4.5); one manned ship per medium and a quarter of the pool cap ashore; only out of view (no player's interest centre within `view_radius` of the dock) and only with a lane worth hunting for a fresh pirate, else again after 48 days. | Reuses the shared rule with the numbers EQUILIBRIUM already gives a broken faction site. The prey check stops a site with nothing to hunt from cycling through refound and starving. |
| PC6 | **New havens form only at found haven sites** (their own site, after it shut). | C22: the sim never makes a site. Bounds the haven count by the sites found. |
| PC7 | **A navy's assault force is its full complement** (`navy_ships x navy_crew x navy_strength`), the port's marines; no ship leaves its timetable and the navy keeps no losses. Looks every 24 days (`assault_every_days`). | Moving a timetabled ship is forbidden (it would jump in view); a landing party from the port is the abstract force. With the p > 2/3 gate the navy always wins outright, so losses would be bookkeeping only. 24 days is S1's census grid. |
| PC8 | **A haven's defence against an assault** is its men ashore plus the crews of its ships in port, at pirate strength and hull; ships at sea do not defend. | A navy that strikes while the fleet is out is the natural counter to a haven; it makes the toll matter (a worn pool is a weak haven). |
| PC9 | **A port raid is a coast raid's third target**, after a player district and a player's train: the port village at the hunted lane's nearer end, if unwatched, under the same roll (T25). Square law against `pop x 0.25` (the conflict engine's `village_defence_per_pop`), gated by p > 2/3; carry 10 items a man (between the bandit thug's 12 and crossbowman's 8), at most 600‰ of each stock (`loot_share_milli`). | Reuses the conflict engine's plunder shape and the world's held-goods path, so goods are conserved exactly. The ships' law is the square law (aimed fire), as for their district raids (I22). |
| PC10 | **News is five new report kinds** in `rtsim-knowledge`, ids 16 to 20 of the range 16 to 23 the talk build reserved for ports and ships: PortRaided 16, Blockade 17 (begun), PortClosed 18, PortReopened 19, HavenFell 20. `who` the haven (the navy's port for 20), `whom` the port's settlement (the haven for 20), `place` the port (the haven for 20), severity 1 (2 for a closure and a fall). Sentiment 0 and no vanilla gossip mapping (no player is `who`). Witnesses: the port and the places in sight, as robbery reports. No report for a refound or a starved haven. | CL-9: all faction news goes in the world; CONFLICT-LIFE §2.17: news of others' fights changes no feeling. Starving and refounding are absences and arrivals, told by the robberies that stop or start. Players hear the kinds by talking to villagers (the talk build's line table for 16 to 23). |
| PC11 | **A hunt that feeds a starving haven again wakes its slots** (`wake_slots`): every slot whose wait is over, laid-up ones included, books its refit at once; short of crew it waits for hires as S8's refits do. `check()` holds that a laid-up slot lies only in a haven that is starving or shut. | A laid-up slot books no refit (PC3), and a starving haven has no hire time to wait for, so without a wake a haven fed again by its one sailing ship would never man the rest. |
| PC12 | **The toll draws on the pool only beyond the crews its empty slots are owed** (T34, as top-ups do); the rest comes off the crew. | Measured with the toll on the whole pool: seeds 9 and 28 of `kill_all_pirates` never manned their empty sky slot within 120 days, because tolls held the pool at 2 to 4 men, below a sky crew of 5. T34 already holds those crews back from top-ups. |
| PC13 | **A fed haven whose last ship is sunk stays fed until `starve_days` after its first slot's refit is due** (`wait_on_refits`). | PC4 says a kill-all is answered by refits. Measured without it: the toll keeps the pool low, so after a kill-all the slots could not be manned before the haven starved, and 26 of 30 harsh kill-alls (pool emptied, P + 1) shut and refounded, all back after 234.5 days [52.8, 254.8] against 52.8 with the lifecycle off. With it, no seed shuts: all back in 52.8 days [52.5, 52.8] (lifecycle off: 52.5 [47.0, 52.8]), and 52.8 both ways after the harsh kill-all. A month of hires mans a sea crew even from an empty pool (29.2 days). |
| PC14 | **A slot short of crew in a fed haven looks again at the turn** (`fed_until`) when hires cannot man it before the haven starves; a starving haven books no hire time (its pool only falls). | Hires stop at the turn (PC2), so a hire time past it would be false; at the turn a hunt begun since may have fed the haven on. |
| PC15 | **Laid up is a lost slot with no refit due** (`Status::Lost { back: LAID_UP }`, `u64::MAX`): crew 0, its crew into the pool (capped as captures are), its captain dead to the books, its hold kept and still held. **A ship in view (embodied) is never laid up and pays no toll.** A late or replayed FIGHT or DEMATERIALIZED report for a ship that is no longer active changes nothing (FIGHT carries no epoch, so the ship's state decides). With `hires_per_day <= 0` the pool is frozen, desertion included (one rate for both). | No new status, so every lost-slot path (census, archive, saves) already handles it. An embodied ship's fights are reported, and taking a ship away in view is forbidden. |
| PC16 | **The haven row holds the lifecycle**: `fed_until`, `shut: Option<Shut { since, back, broken }>` (the design's dormant state), an `epoch`, and counters (`HavenCounts`: toll in thousandths, laid up, shuts, refounds), saved on the row (still `RTS2`). One event kind, `Ev::Refound { haven, epoch }`, is the haven's look: a shut haven has exactly one live look, at `back`; an open haven with no active ship has one at `fed_until`, to shut it at the turn. A look with a stale epoch does nothing. `check()` holds these. | The epoch voids stale looks as plan stamps void ship events. Counters on the row and not in `ShipStats` keep a save with no haven byte for byte as before. |
| PC17 | **A refound relaunches every empty slot** (one per medium) with a new captain, a full crew and a sound hull, resting before it decides; the pool is set to a quarter of the cap, the pressure is kept, and the haven counts as fed from the band's arrival, as at a founding. It counts as a renewal (`renewals` = rebuilds + refounds). | Rule 5's band through S8's launch path. Pressure is the players' mark on the site and outlives the band. |
| PC18 | **In view, for a refound**: a player's live interest leg in the dock's frame within `view_radius` of the dock by the topology distance. **Prey**: a fresh full-crew pirate of one of the site's media finds one of the haven's lanes worth hunting (`huntable`, T35's values). A failed look tries again `base_days` (48) later. | The view test the engine already uses for players, and the pirates' own decision, so a refound sees what its pirates would. |
| PC19 | **`synth::sea_havens(seed, tun)`**, the many-havens test world: an open sea 12,000 blocks square; 12 to 20 ports at least 1,400 apart, every third with a sky dock; 1 or 2 navies at the first ports; 3 to 5 havens (the first, and the second with two navies, 700 blocks off a navy's port; the rest at least 1,000 from ports, 2,500 from other havens and 3,000 from navy ports where they fit), one in three with a sky slot as well. | Worlds where some havens face patrols and some do not, so `unattended_havens_rise_and_fall` can see havens both fall and hold. Every number is a PLACEHOLDER. |
| PC20 | **A lane's heat** is the exponential average of its hazard share with a 30-day time constant (`ShipTunables::blockade_days`), thousandths, stored with its tick on the lane's threat row and moved in closed form at each hazard change (`apply_hazard`, already an event). Built in billionths (PC86). | "Every lane at a haven's full hazard for 30 days" (PORTS-2 (b)) read as a month-long average: hunts come and go in days, so an instant reading would never hold for 30 days and a running average needs no history. |
| PC21 | **Thresholds, against one full-crew pirate of the dock's medium hunting the lane unopposed** (sea 6 x 20 = 120‰, sky 100‰): blockade begun when every lane of the dock is at a quarter or more; closed at a half or more; reopened when no lane is above a quarter and the dock has been closed 30 days (`blockade_min_closed_days`). | "Full hazard" in the option was a haven's hazard, not the 400‰ cap, which one haven's ship (120‰) never reaches. A half means hunted about half of the last month; the quarter-to-half band, the month-long average and the 30-day minimum are the hysteresis Ben asked for. |
| PC22 | **What closes is a dock** (a node and a medium): the lanes touching it carry nothing new (their world edges closed with `World::close_lane`, the `Edge::closed` flag of PC50; consignments already under way land), every merchant service that calls there lays up, and any other lane whose services are all laid up is closed as well. Which services are laid up and which lanes are closed is derived from the ports' states, whatever order they closed in (PC80). | A service is a fixed loop and the network is never re-planned (that would move ships), so a service cannot skip a port; laying the whole service up is the honest consequence, and a lane with no carrier cannot carry flows. |
| PC23 | **A ship in view or with a player aboard is never laid up there**: it keeps its timetable with empty holds until its body is dematerialized (the plugin removes bodies only out of every player's view), then lays up. A ship out of view lays up at once. Nothing is placed or removed in view; no block is touched. Built with the voyage under way keeping its cargo (PC81) and a ship holding promoted cargo laying up once it lands. | UR-1 and "never move a ship in view". A player riding a ship keeps it in view, so he is never dropped in the sea or the sky; the ship sails its last loop and lays up after he leaves it. |
| PC24 | **Reopening relaunches each laid-up ship at its slot's next stay at its service's home dock** (`relaunch_time`, T27, through `Ev::Respawn`), and opens each lane the rule no longer closes (`World::open_lane`, which clears the flag; no capacity is stored or restored, PC50). Consignments then take the lane's lag (PC87). | The existing relaunch path never puts a ship in view mid-lane; the flag leaves the attach-time capacity untouched, so the world's numbers stay exact. |
| PC25 | **Checks**: a dock is checked every 6 days (`blockade_check_days`) while any lane of it has heat above zero, or while it is closed; a dock with cold lanes books nothing. Built with a floor of 1‰ and also while blockaded (PC83). | Bounded by the docks pirates hunt near, not by the network. |

## S8b-4a: a world lane can be closed and reopened (PC50 to PC59)

The world half of a port blockade (PC22). The ships engine will call it in a later slice.

| Id | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| PC50 | **A closed lane is `Edge::closed: bool` (default false), not a capacity of 0.** `cap_slot_milli` is untouched by a closure. This replaces PC22's "capacity set to 0" and PC24's "restores each lane's capacity": a reopen clears the flag and nothing is restored. | `cap_slot_milli <= 0` already means no limit, so 0 cannot mean closed; a flag leaves the attach-time capacity exactly as `attach_lane` computed it. |
| PC51 | **The flag is the last field of the edge's persist record** (`rtsim-travel` `Edge::encode`), and the world's state hash covers it. No format bump, no migration. | Nothing is released (no backward compatibility before release). A hash that left it out would call a closed and an open world equal. |
| PC52 | **API: `World::close_lane(edge: u32, at: Tick) -> bool`, `World::open_lane(edge, at) -> bool`, `World::lane_closed(edge) -> bool`** in `rtsim-world` `lanes.rs`. Each close or open does `run_until(at)` first. | The same shape as `set_lane_hazard`, which the ships engine already calls. |
| PC53 | **Both ends re-allocate at once through `market_event(end, at, Cause::Lane)`**, end `a` then end `b`: the call `set_lane_hazard` and `land_held` use. Not `emit`, which only sends news. | `market_event` re-runs the node's flow allocation and books the changed pipelines' arrivals at the far ends; `emit` leaves the flows as they were. `Cause::Lane` does not force news, so a closure sends no extra packet. |
| PC54 | **Closing and opening are idempotent**: closing a closed lane or opening an open one returns `true` and changes nothing (no flag write, no market event, same state hash). | A blockade check that fires twice must not wake two markets twice. |
| PC55 | **Only a ships' lane closes.** `close_lane` and `open_lane` return `false` and change nothing for a road (`Medium::Road`) or a missing edge. `World::check_lanes` and the snapshot load both reject a closed road. | Roads have no docks to blockade, and the road index and caravans assume an open road. |
| PC56 | **The allocation offers no new goods on a closed edge** (`flows.rs`, in the candidate loop). A direction that was sending (goods or raided share above 0) stays as a candidate with margin -1 and want 0, so its rate winds down to 0 in the same re-allocation; one that was not sending is not a candidate. The ends' demand then goes to their other edges as with any lane that stops. | The existing no-news branch does the same for a destination that has gone quiet, so the grant is exactly 0 and no new rule is needed. |
| PC57 | **Consignments already in a pipeline still arrive, and payments still travel the closed edge.** Payments are computed from the arrivals on the opposite direction (`din.goods`), not from the edge's capacity or the goods candidates, and `set_payments` never reads `closed`. | Coins for goods already shipped must settle or conservation breaks. The test checks a payment pipeline above 0 after the close and an empty road once both lags have run. |
| PC58 | **Other paths that put goods on an edge were checked and need no change.** The only writers of a direction's `goods` and `raid` pipelines are the allocation (`flows.rs`) and its coverage cut, which only scales rates down. `open_exchange` and `add_lane`'s border step only set market lines. `cut_lane_window` (the ships engine's promotion, `Cut`) cuts what is already in a pipeline and adds nothing. Caravans, stops and bands run on roads, which never close. News and messengers are untouched. | A grep of every `goods.entry`, `raid.entry`, `.set(` and `cap_slot_milli` use; the invariant in PC59 backs it. |
| PC59 | **The hazard raid share is a share of the flows, so it falls to 0 with them** (`split_for_raid(0, _)` is `(0, 0)`); the lane's hazard stays set and applies again at the reopen. **Check:** `World::check_lanes()` (every closed edge's goods and raid pipelines run at 0 in both directions) runs at the end of `close_lane` when `check_every_event` is on, and is public for tests. It is not run per event, because between the two ends' events of a closure the far end still sends. A closed edge's goods pipelines hold no new consignment from the next boundary, so once the closure is more than the lag old nothing more than payments is on it. | Cheap (one pass over the edges) and true by PC56. The supplier count is handled in PC60. |
| PC60 | **A neighbour whose every edge to a market is closed is not counted as a supplier** (`count_suppliers` takes the edges). Closing or opening a lane also makes both ends send their news at once (`emit(end, at, true)` after the two `market_event` calls), because the supplier count is in the snapshot but is not a change in kind, so it would not otherwise be sent. | `want_from` asks each live supplier for `ceil(advertised / suppliers)`. A neighbour behind a closed lane keeps news at or above At for the whole closure, so without this the suppliers left were asked for half or less of the want, closing the gap by half per news round. With it, the road supplier is asked for the whole want at its first allocation after the news arrives (test `a_closed_lane_is_no_supplier_and_the_road_supplier_is_asked_for_the_whole_want`). |
| PC61 | **With `barter_in_flows`, payments in kind also travel a closed lane** (PC57 covers coins and items alike: both are `pay` pipelines). `World::check_lanes` checks only the goods and raid pipelines, never `pay`. | A payer that refuses or cannot cover cash pays in an item the exporter needs; the item is owed for goods already shipped, so cutting it would break conservation. Tests: `barter_payments_still_travel_a_closed_lane_and_conserve` (payments in kind are seen on the open and on the closed lane) and `every_consignment_sent_before_the_close_is_paid_for` (with exact payments, the coins paid equal the value of everything landed, to the thousandth). |

## S8b-4b: a blockaded trade port closes and reopens (PC80 to PC89)

The ships engine's half of rule 7, on branch `ports/close` (`crates/rtsim-ships/src/engine/blockade.rs`, tests in `crates/rtsim-ships/tests/blockade.rs`). It closes and opens lanes with S8b-4a's `World::close_lane` and `World::open_lane`.

| Id | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| PC80 | **Only the ports' states are stored; the rest is derived.** A `PortState { blockaded, closed, check, epoch }` per trade dock a hunted lane has touched. A merchant service is laid up when any dock it calls at is closed (`service_laid_up`), since the earliest of those closures (`laid_up_since`). A lane is closed when either end is a closed port, or when it has services and every one is laid up (`lane_shut`). Each closure or reopening runs `resettle`, which lays up or relaunches the services' ships and closes or opens every touched lane's world edge to match. `check()` holds that every lane's edge agrees with the rule, that a laid-up merchant's service is laid up, that a laid-up merchant holds no promoted voyage and no cargo, and that a laid-up service's ship still sailing is in view or holds a promoted voyage. | Ports close and reopen in any order and share services; a rule over stored states gives the same lanes and ships whatever the order, and a restart has nothing to rebuild. The no-cargo invariant was added after a mutation (laying up a promoted ship with empty holds before it landed) passed every test without it. |
| PC81 | **The voyage under way when its port closes keeps its cargo; the service's later voyages carry nothing** (`voyage_laid_up`: a voyage that departs at or after the lay-up). A body in view shows that voyage's consignments and a loot or raid can still promote them; from its next departure the ship sails with empty holds. | Those consignments are already in the world's pipelines and land whatever the ship does (PC57). Emptying the holds at once would show a body in view losing cargo that still lands. PC23's empty holds start at the next voyage. |
| PC82 | **The blockade lifts at an eighth.** An open blockaded port with no lane above an eighth of full is no longer blockaded (`PortChange::Lifted`, no news). `PortState::step` is the check rule as a pure function, applied in the order reopen, lift, blockade, close. | Rule 7 gave the closure a band (half to a quarter) but the blockade none, so a heat hovering at a quarter set and cleared the blockade at every check. Test `a_blockade_cannot_flap`: square waves of share hovering on an eighth, a quarter and a half cross each 89 times in 600 days; the port changes 0, 1 and 2 times. On the blockade coast (seeds 1 to 4, 400 days) the share crosses half 18, 12, 32 and 24 times and the port changes 4, 4, 6 and 4 times. |
| PC83 | **Checks.** Closures are on when `lifecycle` is on and `blockade_days > 0` (`blockades_on`). A trade dock is checked every 6 days while one of its lanes is warm (a share above 0, or a heat above 1‰, `HEAT_FLOOR_MILLI`), or while it is blockaded or closed. A lane's share turning positive in `apply_hazard` books a check at each trade end with none (`book_port_checks`); each check books the next while the rule wants one. One live check per port, stamped by an epoch (`Ev::DockCheck { dock, epoch }`); a stale one only counts as `stale`. | An exponential never reaches 0, so PC25's "heat above zero" would check for ever; the floor ends the checks once the heat rounds to 0‰. Checks go on while blockaded so the lift at an eighth is seen. The cost is one event per 6 days per dock pirates hunt near. |
| PC84 | **A trade port is a village's dock; a haven's dock never closes. A village's sea dock and sky dock are separate ports**, each with its own lanes, full share (by medium) and state. Only lanes with a world edge are weighed. | Rule 7 speaks of a port's dock. The two media are hunted by different pirates and have different full shares. |
| PC85 | **The navy keeps sailing.** Only merchant services lay up; a patrol's ships are never laid up by a closure, its hops never count toward a lane's "every service laid up", and it still sails a closed lane. | A navy patrols to keep the lanes open; one that stopped when its port's lanes were hunted would hand the pirates the sea. The measurement asserts every day that no navy ship is laid up. |
| PC86 | **The heat is stored in billionths of the share** (`LaneThreat::heat_nano`, 1,000,000 per thousandth) with the tick it was last moved (`heat_at`); reads round to thousandths (`LaneThreat::heat`, `Ships::heat_at`). | In thousandths the closed form rounded at every share change, and with shares changing every few days the heat stalled short of its target: a square wave whose heat should swing between 13.5‰ and 16.5‰ settled between 11‰ and 14‰, so a hovering heat never reached the threshold it should. |
| PC87 | **A reopening relaunches each laid-up ship at `relaunch_time(s, at)`** (its slot's next stay at its service's home dock, T27) through `Ev::Respawn`; a respawn that falls due while the service is still laid up keeps the ship in port. The lanes open at once, and the flow allocation offers goods on them again from the next boundary, so consignments take the lane's lag before they land. | The existing relaunch path never puts a ship in view mid-lane. Test `a_blockaded_port_closes_and_reopens`: every laid-up ship active again, nothing on the lanes from ten days after the closure until the reopening, and goods on a lane within 60 days after it. |
| PC88 | **Records.** One new tape kind, `ships.port` (Notable), at the dock: subject the port's node, object the haven of the hottest lane (0 when none), magnitude 1 blockaded, 2 closed, 3 reopened, 4 no longer blockaded. No ReportKinds 17, 18 or 19: `port_news` is a stub, called where each would be sent and commented with its kind. Counters in `ShipStats`: `blockades`, `closures`, `reopenings`, `ships_laid_up_by_closure`, `closed_dock_days` (whole days, credited at the reopening) and `port_checks`. The ports and counters are a trailing section of the resident `RTS2` state, written only once a port has a state or a counter has moved. No format bump, no migration. No census kind `ship.port`: `Ships::census` has no world, and whether a dock is a trade port is the world node's kind, so it would need a new census signature in `rtsim-cli` and the native engine. | Knowledge has no port kinds yet (rule 8); the tape and the notes carry the events meanwhile. Test `blockade_off_changes_nothing`: with `blockade_days = 0` the ships' and the world's state hashes equal the parent commit's (03f9a3f6) on three runs. Test `closures_survive_save_and_restore`: restarts while blockaded, while closed with a promoted ship still sailing, and while closed past the minimum end on the never-saved run's `state_hash`. |
| PC89 | **Limits.** A lane whose services are partly laid up keeps its whole flow. A ship with a player aboard counts as in view (the plugin keeps a ridden body materialized): it is never laid up or despawned while he rides it, keeps its timetable, keeps the cargo of the voyage under way and sails later voyages empty, and lays up only after he leaves it and the plugin dematerializes it out of every player's view. A player standing on its deck is the same case. | Splitting a lane's flow by its sailing services would need the flows to know the timetable. The ridden case is UR-1's and PC23's; test `a_ship_in_view_lays_up_only_after_it_leaves_view` rides a ship through the closure: no `SHIP_DESPAWN`, the ship active and embodied after the closure, its body's cargo empty three days on, laid up after the player leaves. |

**Measured** (`blockades_on_many_seas_100_years` and `_1000_years`, release; `synth::sea_havens` and `synth::coastal`, seeds 1 to 30, nobody watching, closures on and off, sampled daily). Over 1,000 years on `sea_havens` (30,000 sea-years) with closures on: per sea per decade 0.76 blockades, 5.39 closures, 5.39 reopenings and 10.8 ships laid up; closed spells 30.4 days on average, 37 at the longest, and never more than 11.0 days past the last day a lane of the port was above a quarter; 0.86% of port-days closed; 33 of 589 ports ever closed, in 18 of 30 seas; 2.5% fewer hunts than with closures off (86.34 against 88.53 a sea-year); havens open 0.973 of the time (0.980 off); every sea hunts in every decade (399 hunts at the fewest); no navy ship ever laid up; never every sea port of a sea closed at once. Every sky port of a sea was closed at once on 55,905 sea-days, in seas with two or four sky ports (seed 12's two, joined by one lane, on 53,550 of 96,000 days); the navy patrols only the sea. Closures gather in a few ports: in seeds 3, 10, 11 and 12 a port whose lanes are its haven's main prey closes again 17 to 30 days after a reopening on average and spends 50% to 64% of the time closed, and those four seas hold 79% of the closures. Blockades are fewer than closures because a port stays blockaded between closures until its lanes cool below an eighth. Over 100 years: per sea per decade 1.49 blockades, 6.18 closures; spells 30.4 days on average, 37 at the longest; 0.99% of port-days closed (61.6% for the most-closed port); 32 ports ever closed in 17 seas; 2.1% fewer hunts (88.54 against 90.44); havens open 0.982 (0.985 off); 400 hunts in a decade at the fewest. `synth::coastal`, whose navy patrols the lanes, never closes a port in 1,000 years (0.051 blockades per sea per decade; 22.27 hunts a sea-year both ways).
