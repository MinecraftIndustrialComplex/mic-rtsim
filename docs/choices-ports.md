# Choices: havens and ports that rise and fall (S8b)

Slice S8b of EQUILIBRIUM.md (§2.4.6a), started 2026-10-22 on branch `ports/lifecycle` after Ben's
answer to T37: "Huh, shouldn't ports be able to get raided, shut down, have new ones form, etc?"
Each row can be undone. Ben answered PORTS-1 and PORTS-2 on 2026-10-22 (EQUILIBRIUM.md §5.1,
DECISIONS.md): navies break havens (his exception, for havens only, to §2.4.5's rule that only
players break a faction), and trade ports close under blockade and reopen when it lifts (not the
recommendation). Ids: PC1 to PC10 the design, PC11 to PC19 the build of S8b-1, PC20 onward the
blockade and closure.

No backward compatibility before a release: the save format stays `RTS2`, haven rows gain fields
without a migration (no saved world holds a haven: no plugin sends HAVEN_FOUND, T36).

| Id | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| PC1 | **An unwatched hunt pays its toll when the pirate reaches the lane**: the square law of its strength against the defence it judged the lane by (one merchant of the medium plus patrols and escorts, T35's D), `crew x (1 - square_law(A, D))` men in thousandths. The pool makes it good; what the pool lacks comes off the crew in whole men, rounded up. A pirate in view (embodied) pays nothing here: its real fights are reported. | The engine's fights are certain (Lanchester), so the cost of a hunt is the law's, not a roll; T35's D is the fight the pirate itself believed it was taking on, so belief and cost agree. One merchant per hunt is the least a hunt that takes any share must meet. |
| PC2 | **Fed for 30 days** (`ShipTunables::starve_days`): a haven is fed while one of its pirates began a hunt within 30 days; starving, its pool turns toward 0 at the hire rate (`pool e^(-r t / cap)`), one stored tick (`fed_until`) marking the turn. | Six rest-and-decide cycles of a pirate (a day's rest, a fruitless decision, a day's rest...) is long enough that a lane briefly escorted does not starve a haven, short enough that a haven with no prey does not sit manned for years. |
| PC3 | **A pirate is laid up at a fruitless decision while its haven starves**: crew ashore, slot empty with no refit booked. | Without it a starving haven's ships rest in port for ever; laying up at a decision needs no new event. |
| PC4 | **A shut haven** has no active ship and is broken or starving; it books no refit and the census counts it `broken`. A fed haven with every ship sunk is not shut (S8's refits still run). | Keeps S8's kill-all numbers: a player's kill-all is answered by refits, not by refounding. |
| PC5 | **Refound after `min(384, 48 x 2^P)` days** (`ShipTunables::refound`, half-life 96), the camps' rule (§2.4.5); one manned ship per medium and a quarter of the pool cap ashore; only out of view (no player's interest centre within `view_radius` of the dock) and only with a lane worth hunting for a fresh pirate, else again after 48 days. | Reuses the shared rule with the numbers EQUILIBRIUM already gives a broken faction site. The prey check stops a site with nothing to hunt from cycling through refound and starving. |
| PC6 | **New havens form only at found haven sites** (their own site, after it shut). | C22: the sim never makes a site. Bounds the haven count by the sites found. |
| PC7 | **A navy's assault force is its full complement** (`navy_ships x navy_crew x navy_strength`), the port's marines; no ship leaves its timetable and the navy keeps no losses. Looks every 24 days (`assault_every_days`). | Moving a timetabled ship is forbidden (it would jump in view); a landing party from the port is the abstract force. With the p > 2/3 gate the navy always wins outright, so losses would be bookkeeping only. 24 days is S1's census grid. |
| PC8 | **A haven's defence against an assault** is its men ashore plus the crews of its ships in port, at pirate strength and hull; ships at sea do not defend. | A navy that strikes while the fleet is out is the natural counter to a haven; it makes the toll matter (a worn pool is a weak haven). |
| PC9 | **A port raid is a coast raid's third target**, after a player district and a player's train: the port village at the hunted lane's nearer end, if unwatched, under the same roll (T25). Square law against `pop x 0.25` (the conflict engine's `village_defence_per_pop`), gated by p > 2/3; carry 10 items a man (between the bandit thug's 12 and crossbowman's 8), at most 600‰ of each stock (`loot_share_milli`). | Reuses the conflict engine's plunder shape and the world's held-goods path, so goods are conserved exactly. The ships' law is the square law (aimed fire), as for their district raids (I22). |
| PC10 | **News is five new report kinds** in `rtsim-knowledge`, ids 16 to 20 of the range 16 to 23 the talk build reserved for ports and ships: PortRaided 16, Blockade 17 (begun), PortClosed 18, PortReopened 19, HavenFell 20. `who` the haven (the navy's port for 20), `whom` the port's settlement (the haven for 20), `place` the port (the haven for 20), severity 1 (2 for a closure and a fall). Sentiment 0 and no vanilla gossip mapping (no player is `who`). Witnesses: the port and the places in sight, as robbery reports. No report for a refound or a starved haven. | CL-9: all faction news goes in the world; CONFLICT-LIFE §2.17: news of others' fights changes no feeling. Starving and refounding are absences and arrivals, told by the robberies that stop or start. Players hear the kinds by talking to villagers (the talk build's line table for 16 to 23). |
| PC11 | **A hunt that feeds a starving haven again wakes its slots** (`wake_slots`): every slot whose wait is over, laid-up ones included, books its refit at once; short of crew it waits for hires as S8's refits do. `check()` holds that a laid-up slot lies only in a haven that is starving or shut. | A laid-up slot books no refit (PC3), and a starving haven has no hire time to wait for, so without a wake a haven fed again by its one sailing ship would never man the rest. |
| PC12 | **The toll draws on the pool only beyond the crews its empty slots are owed** (T34, as top-ups do); the rest comes off the crew. | Measured with the toll on the whole pool: seeds 9 and 28 of `kill_all_pirates` never manned their empty sky slot within 120 days, because tolls held the pool at 2 to 4 men, below a sky crew of 5. T34 already holds those crews back from top-ups. |
| PC13 | **A fed haven whose last ship is sunk stays fed until `starve_days` after its first slot's refit is due** (`wait_on_refits`). | PC4 says a kill-all is answered by refits. Measured without it: the toll keeps the pool low, so after a kill-all the slots could not be manned before the haven starved, and 26 of 30 harsh kill-alls (pool emptied, P + 1) shut and refounded, all back after 234.5 days [52.8, 254.8] against 52.8 with the lifecycle off. With it, no seed shuts: all back in 52.8 days [52.5, 52.8] (lifecycle off: 52.5 [47.0, 52.8]), and 52.8 both ways after the harsh kill-all. A month of hires mans a sea crew even from an empty pool (29.2 days). |
| PC14 | **A slot short of crew in a fed haven looks again at the turn** (`fed_until`) when hires cannot man it before the haven starves; a starving haven books no hire time (its pool only falls). | Hires stop at the turn (PC2), so a hire time past it would be false; at the turn a hunt begun since may have fed the haven on. |
| PC15 | **Laid up is a lost slot with no refit due** (`Status::Lost { back: LAID_UP }`, `u64::MAX`): crew 0, its crew into the pool (capped as captures are), its captain dead to the books, its hold kept and still held. **A ship in view (embodied) is never laid up and pays no toll.** A late or replayed FIGHT or DEMATERIALIZED report for a ship that is no longer active changes nothing (FIGHT carries no epoch, so the ship's state decides). With `hires_per_day <= 0` the pool is frozen, desertion included (one rate for both). | No new status, so every lost-slot path (census, archive, saves) already handles it. An embodied ship's fights are reported, and taking a ship away in view is forbidden. |
| PC16 | **The haven row holds the lifecycle**: `fed_until`, `shut: Option<Shut { since, back, broken }>` (the design's dormant state), an `epoch`, and counters (`HavenCounts`: toll in thousandths, laid up, shuts, refounds), saved on the row (still `RTS2`). One event kind, `Ev::Refound { haven, epoch }`, is the haven's look: a shut haven has exactly one live look, at `back`; an open haven with no active ship has one at `fed_until`, to shut it at the turn. A look with a stale epoch does nothing. `check()` holds these. | The epoch voids stale looks as plan stamps void ship events. Counters on the row and not in `ShipStats` keep a save with no haven byte for byte as before. |
| PC17 | **A refound relaunches every empty slot** (one per medium) with a new captain, a full crew and a sound hull, resting before it decides; the pool is set to a quarter of the cap, the pressure is kept, and the haven counts as fed from the band's arrival, as at a founding. It counts as a renewal (`renewals` = rebuilds + refounds). | Rule 5's band through S8's launch path. Pressure is the players' mark on the site and outlives the band. |
| PC18 | **In view, for a refound**: a player's live interest leg in the dock's frame within `view_radius` of the dock by the topology distance. **Prey**: a fresh full-crew pirate of one of the site's media finds one of the haven's lanes worth hunting (`huntable`, T35's values). A failed look tries again `base_days` (48) later. | The view test the engine already uses for players, and the pirates' own decision, so a refound sees what its pirates would. |
| PC19 | **`synth::sea_havens(seed, tun)`**, the many-havens test world: an open sea 12,000 blocks square; 12 to 20 ports at least 1,400 apart, every third with a sky dock; 1 or 2 navies at the first ports; 3 to 5 havens (the first, and the second with two navies, 700 blocks off a navy's port; the rest at least 1,000 from ports, 2,500 from other havens and 3,000 from navy ports where they fit), one in three with a sky slot as well. | Worlds where some havens face patrols and some do not, so `unattended_havens_rise_and_fall` can see havens both fall and hold. Every number is a PLACEHOLDER. |
| PC20 | **A lane's heat** is the exponential average of its hazard share with a 30-day time constant (`ShipTunables::blockade_days`), thousandths, stored with its tick on the lane's threat row and moved in closed form at each hazard change (`apply_hazard`, already an event). | "Every lane at a haven's full hazard for 30 days" (PORTS-2 (b)) read as a month-long average: hunts come and go in days, so an instant reading would never hold for 30 days and a running average needs no history. |
| PC21 | **Thresholds, against one full-crew pirate of the dock's medium hunting the lane unopposed** (sea 6 x 20 = 120‰, sky 100‰): blockade begun when every lane of the dock is at a quarter or more; closed at a half or more; reopened when no lane is above a quarter and the dock has been closed 30 days (`blockade_min_closed_days`). | "Full hazard" in the option was a haven's hazard, not the 400‰ cap, which one haven's ship (120‰) never reaches. A half means hunted about half of the last month; the quarter-to-half band, the month-long average and the 30-day minimum are the hysteresis Ben asked for. |
| PC22 | **What closes is a dock** (a node and a medium): the lanes touching it carry nothing new (the world edge's capacity set to 0; consignments already under way land), every service that calls there lays up, and any other lane left with no active service carries nothing. | A service is a fixed loop and the network is never re-planned (that would move ships), so a service cannot skip a port; laying the whole service up is the honest consequence, and a lane with no carrier cannot carry flows. |
| PC23 | **A ship in view or with a player aboard is never laid up there**: it keeps its timetable with empty holds until its body is dematerialized (the plugin removes bodies only out of every player's view), then lays up. A ship out of view lays up at once. Nothing is placed or removed in view; no block is touched. | UR-1 and "never move a ship in view". A player riding a ship keeps it in view, so he is never dropped in the sea or the sky; the ship sails its last loop and lays up after he leaves it. |
| PC24 | **Reopening relaunches each laid-up ship at its slot's next stay at its service's home dock** (`relaunch_time`, T27), and restores each lane's capacity to what `attach_lane` computed from its active services. | The existing relaunch path never puts a ship in view mid-lane; storing the attach-time capacity keeps the world's numbers exact. |
| PC25 | **Checks**: a dock is checked every 6 days (`blockade_check_days`) while any lane of it has heat above zero, or while it is closed; a dock with cold lanes books nothing. | Bounded by the docks pirates hunt near, not by the network. |
| PC40 | **The target end**: of the hunted lane's two docks the one nearer the raiding pirate's haven dock, by straight distance through the haven's frame's topology (`Ships::sdist`; a dock in another frame is never near, though a haven hunts only lanes of its own frame and a lane's docks share it), the lane's `a` on a tie. It must be a trade port: a node of kind `Village` (never a `Haven` node, never a `PlayerBase`). If the nearer end is not, the other end is tried; if neither is, there is no raid. A trade port that is nearer but watched, too strong or empty is not replaced by the far end: the raid is declined (`raid_dock` is public and tested: with every other village too strong, and with a player at every third dock, each raid still hits the nearer end; with the nearer end made a player base or a haven the far end is the target, and with neither there is none). | The text says "the port village at the hunted lane's nearer end". The haven is where the pirates sail from, so its nearer end is the village they can reach first. The far-end fallback covers a lane with a non-village end (a haven's dock, a player base's), so a raid never lands on one. |
| PC41 | **The belief gate is the hunt's own value.** A is `strength(s)` at the moment of the raid (crew after the toll, times its hull), D is the village's `pop x port_village_defence_per_pop` (a new tunable, default 0.25, the conflict engine's `village_defence_per_pop`; the population is the settlement's, as `raid_node` reads it). The pirate goes if `p x hunt_loot_value - (1 - p) x hunt_crew_value > 0` with `p = A² / (A² + D²)`, which is `p > 2/3` with the 3 and 6. The code factors `hunt_value` into `win_belief` and `belief_value` and the raid reuses both, so a change to the hunt's values moves the raid with it. | One belief for every fight a pirate picks (T35, PC1). A full sea crew (A = 6) raids a village up to 16 people, a sky crew (A = 5) up to 14; the coast's ports (30 to 72) are never raided at the defaults, a sea of havens' ports (12) are. |
| PC42 | **The losses are the toll's.** `crew x (1 - square_law(A, D))` in thousandths, a certain law with no roll, booked by the same code as the toll (`book_loss`, factored out of `pay_toll` with its behaviour unchanged): the pool ashore beyond the crews owed to empty slots first (PC12), then the crew in whole men rounded up. They are counted in `ShipStats::port_raid_lost_milli`, not in the haven's `toll_milli` (which the toll tests hold exactly). A crew paid to its last man loses its ship (no killer, no pressure) and the raid takes nothing and is not taped, but it counts: `ShipStats::port_raids` is every raid fought, so `port_raid_lost_milli / port_raids` is the men a raid costs. With p above 2/3 the loss is under 29% of the crew. | PC12 measured that taking tolls from the whole pool starves empty slots; a raid is the same kind of cost. Keeping it out of `toll_milli` keeps PC1's tests exact. |
| PC43 | **The loot** is `survivors x port_raid_carry` items (default 10, between the bandit thug's 12 and the crossbowman's 8; 0 turns port raids off), `survivors` the crew after the losses. **Coins are taken like every other good**: the caps cover every good the market stocks, currency included, within the same carry and the same 600‰ cap, as `raid_node` in the conflict engine does (a reversible choice of the S8b build, 2026-10-22, not one of Ben's: a treasury is what a raider wants). The carry is scaled down so the loot's volume fits the room left under `pirate_hold_slots`, and then trimmed exactly (PC71). It is split over the goods in proportion to each good's cap, `stock x loot_share_milli / 1000`, by `rtsim_industry::reconcile::apportion`, and taken by `World::stock_to_held` into the pirate's `cargo`. The market is read at `now` (the engine and the world are run to the event's time first), so a raid never takes more than 600‰ of a good's stock. A hold the loot fills ends the hunt (`check_hold`, as after a fight). | Reuses the conflict engine's plunder shape and the held-goods path, so the haven's landing (`land_held`) and a sinking (`destroy_held`) carry the goods, coins too, unchanged and the world's conservation check holds with no new accounting. `a_port_raid_conserves_goods` asserts that coins are among what was taken and that they balance like the rest. |
| PC44 | **Unwatched**: no player's live interest leg within `view_radius` in the port dock's frame at the moment of the raid (`point_in_view`, which `dock_in_view` is now a call of; same rule as PC18). A pirate that is embodied (in view) never raids a port, as it pays no toll (PC15), and with `lifecycle` off there are no port raids (S8's behaviour). | Nothing happens to a village under a player's eye that the player could not have affected, and a pirate in view has real fights, not abstract ones. |
| PC45 | **Records.** `ShipStats::{port_raids, items_from_ports, port_raid_lost_milli}` are appended to the end of the stats vector and `ShipLedger::looted_from_ports` to the end of the ledger's maps, saved as the others (still `RTS2`, no migration). One `SHIPS_RAID` tape event at the port's dock, `who` the ship, `object` the village's key, severity Notable when it took anything, text "raids port village K from lane N: M items"; a `port raid` log note. `check()` holds, for each good, that what pirates took from ports is at most what landed at havens, is aboard, sank or was taken by players (the ledger cannot say which hold a good came from once it is mixed with merchants' loot); in a run nobody watches the identity is exact (`a_port_raid_conserves_goods`). Every logged raid also carries what it took and the village's whole stock, in thousandths, for the measurement. No knowledge report: ReportKind PortRaided (16) comes with the knowledge build, and the record call is the parent's to add. | The ledger has no source split, so the exact identity needs a run with no merchant loot, which the tests build; the inequality is what `check()` can hold anywhere. |
| PC46 | **Measured** (`port_raids_measured`, release, seeds 1 to 30, 10 model years, carry 10 against 0, after the merge of S8b-1's review fixes and the fixes of PC70 and PC71): on `synth::sea_havens`, 15.5 port raids a year a sea (every raid fought), 52.1 items a raid (6 men carry at most 60), 0.82 men lost a raid, a raid takes 0.253 of the village's stock on average (largest 0.600, the cap; 4,656 raids, shares in thousandths of the whole stock), hunts 83.7 a year (92.5 with carry 0), haven manned share 0.961 (0.984). On `synth::coastal` no raid at the defaults (its ports hold 30 to 72); with every port set to 12 people, 1.14 raids a year, 53.0 items, 0.034 of the stock (largest 0.049), 0.91 men a raid, hunts 23.6 a year (38.6), manned 1.000 (1.000). | Facts for the doc's Built line. Fewer hunts follow because a raided village's flows shrink, so fewer lanes are worth hunting (`vol <= 0` in `huntable`), and because raids cost crew. An earlier reading of 0.625 as the largest share was the test's own flooring (whole items over whole stock, each floored), not a stale market read. |
| PC47 | **Nothing is pinned.** No existing test changed: every S1 and S8 test passes unchanged (the coast's ports are too strong to raid, and the sea-of-havens tests pass with port raids on). | The run showed no test depends on village stocks that a port raid moves. |
| PC48 | **`port_raid_carry = 0` is the parent's behaviour**, shown by an A/B. The gate is the first line of `port_raid`, after the coast raid's one roll (no new roll, no new draw). Against `origin/main` at 966d7a93 (S8b-1 landed), built from `git archive | tar --touch -x` with a reflinked target (the test binary holds no "raids port village"), and this branch with `port_raid_carry` 0: the same world `state_hash` and the same engine counters (events, hunts, coast raids, sinkings, bored, rebuilds) on `synth::coastal` and `synth::sea_havens`, seeds 1 to 12, 150 days. `port_raids_off_changes_nothing` holds the in-tree form: a declined raid (strong villages) and carry 0 are the same run, byte for byte, and on the coast carry 10 and 0 are the same run. | The ships' `state_hash` hashes the save bytes, which gained three stat words and a ledger map, so the A/B compares the world's hash and the counters. A first A/B (against 4c1b010a) seemed to show nondeterminism on `sea_havens`: `git archive | tar -x` stamps files with the commit's time, older than the reflinked target's fingerprints, so cargo reused this branch's compiled crates in the "parent" copy. It was a build-cache artifact, not a nondeterminism. |
| PC49 | **A raid that finds no target does not retry**: the coast raid keeps its one roll (T25, bent by Aggressive), and its target order is a player district, a player's train, then the port village. | The text's "a quarter of hunts open with a coast raid": one roll, three targets. |
| PC70 | **No fight for nothing.** Before the fight a raid needs something to take: the village's caps (`stock x loot_share_milli / 1000` over every good) must reach above 0 and the hold must have room above 0. Otherwise there is no raid: no losses, no counter, no tape, no note. | The review found that an emptied village or a full hold would still cost the crew its toll for nothing. The gate order is: nearer end, unwatched, belief, something to take, then the fight. |
| PC71 | **The hold is filled exactly to its capacity.** Each good in the loot may add only as many whole thousandths of a slot as are left, measured as the hold measures itself: the sum over the merged cargo of `amount / stack` (a floor per good). The previous trim ignored what the hold already held, so rounding in the merged measure could leave it a sliver over. `a_raid_fills_a_part_full_hold_exactly_to_its_capacity` builds part-full holds (a hunt has one port raid and the hold is emptied at home, so the engine alone never meets one) and fails if the held amounts are ignored; `a_hold_never_passes_its_capacity_and_a_sunk_pirate_loses_its_port_loot` checks every pirate's hold every quarter day with a one-slot hold. | The "hold is full" logic and the raid must agree on the measure, or a raid could end a hold that is over the line it ends hunts by. |
